# Evidence brief: a detection score is not a finding

**Proposal, for reuse. Not adopted policy.** Sources and limits: `audit-memorandum.md`. Read 2026-11-18.

**Rule.** A score or highlight from a tool that classifies text as generated or altered by artificial intelligence is not, by itself, a finding of academic misconduct and is not, by itself, a grade penalty. The institution still has to meet its own burden of proof with evidence independent of the score, give the student that evidence, and say in writing how it weighed the student’s response. The rule does not ban detectors, does not decide whether any named student used one, and does not make the student produce private search history.

**Why this rule does not wait on a new study.** Turnitin’s guide, updated 2026-11-03, says its percentage “should not be used as the sole basis for action or a definitive grading measure.” Its claim of a false-positive rate under 1 percent is its own target, for documents over 20 percent AI writing, and it changed the English model in October 2026. OpenAI withdrew its classifier on 2023-07-20 after that classifier labeled human text as AI-written 9 percent of the time on OpenAI’s test. Liang and colleagues, *Patterns* (2023), found a 61.3 percent average false-positive rate for seven detectors on 91 human-written TOEFL essays. Weber-Wulff and colleagues (2023) tested fourteen tools, including Turnitin; every tool scored under 80 percent accuracy. Those studies do not measure today’s models. They are enough to reject treating the score as the whole case. Testing current students would be human-subjects research and is not proposed.

**What has been decided.**

*Newby v. Adelphi University*, 2026 NY Slip Op 26021 (Sup. Ct. Nassau County, Jan. 28, 2026): the violation is annulled and the record expunged because Adelphi did not follow its own rules. The court relied on the professor’s later email, the lack of an advisor, the failure to consider contrary detector results under a preponderance standard, and the same official hearing the appeal. The court did not decide whether the essay was machine-written. News reports that say the court found he did not use AI go beyond the judgment.

*Kato v. Palo Alto Unified School District*, No. 5:26-cv-04078 (N.D. Cal.): all claims dismissed by stipulation on 2026-09-02. No merits ruling. Reported allegations of a Turnitin flag and a lower grade remain allegations.

*Rignol v. Yale University*, No. 3:25-cv-00159 (D. Conn.): no merits judgment identified. The complaint alleges an F, a one-year suspension, and biased use of GPTZero. A 2026-07-24 order ended a motion to dismiss because a required pre-filing conference was missed. That order does not decide the claims.

Australian Catholic University: no judgment found. ABC News (2025-10-09) reported internal counts of nearly 6,000 misconduct referrals in 2024, about 90 percent AI-related. Deputy Vice-Chancellor Tania Broadley told the ABC the figure was “substantially overstated,” that about one-quarter of referrals were dismissed, and that sole-Turnitin cases were dismissed immediately. ABC reported documents showing sole reliance and a later decision to drop the tool. Neither account is a judgment.

**UCLA, and the same gap elsewhere.** The Interim 2026 Student Conduct Code already prohibits unauthorized or unattributed AI use, requires the University to prove a violation by a preponderance, and provides notice, an advisor, and an appeal. Academic Senate Regulation A-306 already requires a report and a Deferred Report while suspected cheating is pending. Those texts, and systemwide PACAOS 102.01, do not say what a detection score is worth. UCLA sample syllabus language says an instructor may use a detector and treat “confirmed” use as dishonesty, without defining confirmation. UCSA’s resolution of 2025-01-02 asks for syllabus language only. Education Code section 33328.5 requires a K-12 model policy. It binds neither the University nor a district. A California charter policy adopted 2026-10-13 already says detection software should not be the sole basis for discipline or a grade penalty.

**Three provisions. No others.** (1) The score is not the sole basis for a conduct finding or for a grade penalty imposed for suspected unauthorized AI use. (2) Before either consequence, the student receives the course rule, the tool, the date, the output, and a chance to respond; the decision states the independent evidence. (3) A prohibition is stated before the work begins, and coursework is not uploaded to a detector the institution has not approved. At UCLA, the Academic Senate can act on grades. The Chancellor can act on the conduct code, after consultation and Office of the President review. A student-government resolution can request that text. It cannot enact it.
