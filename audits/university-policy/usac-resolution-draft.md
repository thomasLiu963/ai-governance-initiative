# Draft USAC resolution

**PROPOSAL. Not sponsored, not submitted, and not placed on an agenda.**

This draft follows the clause order of a resolution posted by USAC and the sponsor block in *The USAC Resolution Guide*. Sponsor lines are blank because no Council member has been asked. Bylaws Article VI, Section A.5.c requires at least three members of Council as sponsors. Bylaws Article VI, Section A.5.e says no author or sponsor name shall be included in any published resolution. The guide and posted resolutions include sponsor names in the Council text. This draft keeps the names in the Council text, as blanks, and they should be omitted from any later public advertisement if Section A.5.e is applied to that publication.

The November 24, 2026 meeting is not a placed hearing for this text. See the memorandum for the deadline, quorum, and vote rules. Nothing in this file authorizes anyone to email the Internal Vice President or any other officer.

A RESOLUTION CALLING FOR EVIDENCE RULES WHEN A GENERATIVE-AI DETECTION SCORE IS USED IN AN ACADEMIC MISCONDUCT FINDING OR A GRADE PENALTY

Primary Sponsor:

[Name], [Council office] — not obtained

Co-sponsors:

[Name], [Council office] — not obtained

[Name], [Council office] — not obtained

WHEREAS, the Interim 2026 UCLA Student Conduct Code already provides that, unless the faculty member specifies otherwise, submitted work must be the student’s own or must acknowledge its source, and it already treats unauthorized or prohibited use of artificial intelligence to compose or complete an assignment as cheating and unattributed AI-composed work as plagiarism;1 and,

WHEREAS, the same Code places the burden on the University to prove a violation by a preponderance of the evidence, requires written notice and an opportunity to inspect relevant documents, permits an advisor, and provides an appeal, while a hearing on responsibility is reserved for cases in which the proposed sanction is suspension or dismissal and the student contests responsibility;2 and,

WHEREAS, Divisional Regulation A-306 already provides that the instructor determines the grade from the student’s achievement, that suspected plagiarism or cheating is reported for disciplinary proceedings, and that the grade Deferred Report is assigned until those proceedings are completed;3 and,

WHEREAS, neither the Interim 2026 Student Conduct Code, Divisional Regulation A-306, nor the systemwide ground for discipline at PACAOS 102.01 states whether a percentage, highlight, or other output of a generative-AI detection tool is sufficient evidence of misconduct or sufficient basis for a grade penalty;4 and,

WHEREAS, Turnitin’s own AI-writing guide, updated November 3, 2026, states that Turnitin does not make a determination of misconduct and that the percentage on its AI writing indicator “should not be used as the sole basis for action or a definitive grading measure by instructors”;5 and,

WHEREAS, published evaluations show that detector error is real, uneven, and specific to the tool and the year of the test, including a 2023 study in *Patterns* reporting an average false-positive rate of 61.3 percent for seven detectors on 91 human-written TOEFL essays, and a 2023 study in the *International Journal for Educational Integrity* concluding that fourteen detection tools were neither accurate nor reliable, while OpenAI withdrew its own text classifier on July 20, 2023 because of low accuracy; these studies are not a measurement of any vendor’s current model, and this Council does not adopt a numerical false-positive rate as a finding of fact;6 and,

WHEREAS, in *Matter of Newby v. Adelphi University*, 2026 NY Slip Op 26021 (Sup. Ct. Nassau County Jan. 28, 2026), the court annulled an academic-integrity violation and ordered the related record expunged because the university did not follow its own rules, including by failing to consider the student’s contrary evidence under a preponderance standard and by assigning the appeal to the same official who issued the determination; the judgment does not decide whether the essay was written by a machine;7 and,

WHEREAS, *Kato v. Palo Alto Unified School District*, No. 5:26-cv-04078 (N.D. Cal.), was dismissed by stipulation on September 2, 2026, without a ruling on the merits, and *Rignol v. Yale University*, No. 3:25-cv-00159 (D. Conn.), is a pending civil action in which this Council has identified no merits judgment; allegations in those cases are not findings;8 and,

WHEREAS, the University of California Student Association on January 2, 2025, called for artificial-intelligence guidance in course syllabi, and that resolution does not state an evidentiary rule for detection scores;9 and,

WHEREAS, California Education Code section 33328.5 directs the development of a model policy for local educational agencies and charter schools on the safe use of artificial intelligence, including academic integrity, and a California charter-school policy adopted October 13, 2026, states that AI detection software should not be the sole basis for disciplinary action or a grade penalty; that statute does not bind the University of California, and this resolution does not purport to bind any school district;10 and,

WHEREAS, sample syllabus language published by UCLA Humanities Technology invites an instructor to submit student writing to an AI writing detector and to treat “confirmed” use as academic dishonesty, without defining what confirmation consists of or restating the Deferred Report sequence in Regulation A-306;11 therefore,

THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that it is the opinion of the Undergraduate Students Association Council that a score, percentage, highlight, or other output of a generative-AI detection tool shall not be the sole basis for a finding of responsibility under the UCLA Student Conduct Code, and shall not be the sole basis for a grade or other academic penalty imposed because a student is suspected of unauthorized generative-AI use; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Council calls on the Los Angeles Division of the Academic Senate to consider an amendment to Divisional Regulation A-306, or a legislative ruling, providing that: when an instructor suspects unauthorized generative-AI use, the instructor reports the suspicion and assigns the grade Deferred Report until proceedings are completed; a grade penalty for that suspicion is imposed only after a finding that identifies evidence other than a detection output; if the proceedings do not determine that the student engaged in the suspected misconduct, the instructor grades the work without a penalty based on that suspicion; and the student, before any such finding, receives the course rule at issue, the name of any detection tool, the date it was used, the output relied upon, and a meaningful opportunity to respond, including with drafts or other process evidence; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Council calls on the Chancellor, in consultation with students, faculty, staff, and the Office of Student Conduct, and subject to the review by the Office of the President that the Student Conduct Code already requires before an amendment is adopted, to consider adding to the Code a provision that: a generative-AI detection output does not, by itself, meet the preponderance standard; the reviewer states the independent evidence in writing and addresses the student’s response; and student coursework is not uploaded to a generative-AI detection service that the campus has not approved for that purpose; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Council calls on the Academic Senate and the offices that publish teaching guidance to ask instructors to state, before the relevant work is undertaken, whether generative-AI use is permitted, restricted, or prohibited for that work, and to withdraw sample language that treats an unspecified “confirmed” detector result as academic dishonesty; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the primary sponsor shall transmit this resolution and the accompanying one-page evidence brief to the Chair of the Los Angeles Division of the Academic Senate, the Office of Student Conduct, the Vice Chancellor for Student Affairs, and the University of California Student Association, with a request that UCSA consider a systemwide companion limited to this evidentiary rule and not a restatement of its January 2, 2025 syllabus resolution; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Council expresses support for California local educational agencies and charter schools that adopt, in their own policies, the limit that AI detection software not be the sole basis for discipline or a grade penalty, and directs no demand to any school district; and,

BE IT FINALLY RESOLVED, that this resolution expresses the opinion of the current Council under the USA Bylaws, creates no new student-conduct offense, finds no named student responsible or not responsible, and does not assert that any court has held a detection product to be scientifically invalid.
