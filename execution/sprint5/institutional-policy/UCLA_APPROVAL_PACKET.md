## What passage is, and what it leaves unchanged

A yes vote records the opinion of this Council in the posted minutes. USA Bylaws Article VI, Section A.5, in `Bylaws-Master-2026-Revision_updated-102926.pdf`, is the source of that limit, of the three-sponsor rule, and of the vote rule.

The Interim 2026 Student Conduct Code changes when the Chancellor adopts an amendment after the consultation and the Office of the President review the Code already requires. Divisional Regulation A-306 changes when the Los Angeles Division of the Academic Senate amends it or issues a legislative ruling. This resolution asks those bodies to consider text. The Council does not enact the text, does not decide a student's case, and does not create a new conduct offense.

The Academic Senate's AI Principles for Instruction, announced October 19, 2026, are faculty guidance. They are a decision that already happened. They are not this resolution, and they are not a substitute for A-306 or the Conduct Code.

## Rules that control the filing

Opened November 20 from the files linked on https://www.usac.ucla.edu/documents/bylaws, https://www.usac.ucla.edu/documents/guiding-docs, and https://www.usac.ucla.edu/documents/misc.

**Sponsors.** Article VI, Section A.5.c: "Resolutions must have at least three (3) members of Council listed as sponsors." The Resolution Guide says the author plus at least two other USAC members. The guide cites the May 9, 2023 bylaws. The October 29, 2026 bylaws are the text that governs. Appointed and ex-officio members do not vote, under Constitution Article II, Section D.1. They are not used as one of the three sponsors. No current member has agreed to sponsor this text.

**Vote.** Article VI, Section A.5.d: "Resolutions must receive a majority vote of the entire voting membership of the Council to be approved." Constitution Article II, Section D.1: the President votes only in a tie or where the vote will affect a constitutionally required two-thirds or three-fourths. The other elected officers vote on all questions. This packet does not turn "entire voting membership" into a required number of yes votes, because the bylaws do not say whether the President is in the denominator on a vote the President does not cast.

**Quorum.** During the academic year, two-thirds of the officers constitute a quorum. Two-thirds of the fifteen elected offices is ten if every seat is filled. Proxy attendance and proxy voting are not recognized. If a quorum is absent, those present sit as a committee of the whole and cannot pass a resolution.

**Deadline.** Article VI, Section A.2.i: the officer submits the item to the Internal Vice President's electronic mailbox no later than 12:00 p.m. three school days before the meeting. "Any items received after the deadline will not be included on the Agenda." The guide's Friday 3:00 p.m. instruction is the 2023 text. For the January 12 meeting, Friday, January 8, at 3:00 p.m. is after the bylaw deadline. The email goes to ivp@usac.ucla.edu by Thursday, January 7, 2027, at noon.

**Packet.** Action items need their written text in the packet, or they are discussion items unless unanimous consent waives that requirement. The public agenda is due by 7:00 p.m. two school days before the meeting. For January 12, that time is Friday, January 8, 2027, at 7:00 p.m. Officers receive the agenda by 5:00 p.m. that same day.

**Floor addition.** Article VI, Section A.3.d: an action item is added when the agenda is approved only if waiting until the next meeting would materially harm its purpose. Section A.3.c requires a two-thirds vote when a quorum is present. This resolution is not written for a floor motion.

**After passage.** Article VI, Section A.5.f: the main sponsors give two updates, at the second or third later meeting and at the sixth or seventh, in writing on the Resolution Action Item Updates section and orally. The Internal Vice President adds a special presentation if they miss those updates. A missed update can cost a stipend check. The Council can cancel the updates by majority vote. The resolution assigns transmission to the primary sponsor so the first update has a defined act to report.

**Names.** Article VI, Section A.5.e: "No author or sponsor's name shall be included in any published resolution." The guide ties a similar rule to a paid advertisement. Posted resolution PDFs on the USAC site do include sponsor names. This draft keeps sponsor lines in the Council packet, blank until each person has said yes, and leaves the names out of any later public advertisement.

The bylaws do not define "school day." The January 7 deadline uses the registrar's Winter Quarter 2027 table: instruction begins Monday, January 4, and Martin Luther King Jr. Day is Monday, January 18. January 7, January 8, and January 11 are the three school days before January 12. The winter campus closure is "December TBD" and does not cover those three days on the calendar that was opened.

## Three-sponsor acquisition

The barrier is the three yeses. The text is not the barrier. One office is asked at a time. A reply that is interested, or that asks to see the text, is not a sponsor line. A name is typed into the resolution only after that member writes that they will sponsor it.

1. Angela Ledesma-Grattarola, Academic Affairs Commissioner. Bylaws Article IV, Section F.1.a assigns that office to represent students on educational policy and academic affairs to the administration and faculty. Section F.1.b and F.1.c assign student appointments to Academic Senate committees and a seat, or a designated representative, on the Academic Senate Executive Board committees. She is the officer who can carry the Senate ask after passage. She is asked first, and she is asked to be the primary sponsor.
2. Cecilia Choy, External Vice President, after Ledesma-Grattarola has said yes or has said no. Constitution Article III assigns the External Vice President to assist with advocacy to off-campus entities. The resolution asks UCSA to consider a systemwide companion. Choy is UCLA's voting member of that board on the November 19 reading of the UCSA roster. She is a sponsor of the USAC resolution. She is not asked, in this packet, to file a separate UCSA resolution. The current UCSA bylaws were not opened, and the May 2013 PDF is not treated as the vote rule.
3. One General Representative, after the first two answers are in. The order is Evan Salazar, then Pranaya Vijay, then Susie Turner. A General Representative votes on ordinary questions and does not have a commission portfolio that makes this ask look misdirected. The President is not asked to fill a sponsor seat, because she does not vote on an ordinary resolution except in a tie. The Internal Vice President is not asked to fill a sponsor seat, because her mailbox is the filing channel.

If the Academic Affairs Commissioner declines, the External Vice President is asked to be the primary sponsor, and two General Representatives are asked to co-sponsor. The same explicit-yes rule applies. The other commissioners are asked only if three voting members have not agreed by Friday, January 1, 2027. That leaves the week of January 4 to put the text in the primary sponsor's hands before noon on January 7.

Do not email all fifteen offices at once. A pile of identical emails is how a short resolution becomes a staffing problem for the Internal Vice President before it has a sponsor.

## Exact resolution

Sponsor lines stay blank until each member has agreed in writing.

```
A RESOLUTION CALLING FOR HUMAN REVIEW, NOTICE, AND A CHANCE TO CHALLENGE
WHEN AN ARTIFICIAL-INTELLIGENCE OUTPUT IS USED IN A CONSEQUENTIAL
STUDENT DECISION

Primary Sponsor:

[Name], [Council office] — not obtained

Co-sponsors:

[Name], [Council office] — not obtained

[Name], [Council office] — not obtained

WHEREAS, the Interim 2026 UCLA Student Conduct Code already treats
unauthorized or prohibited use of artificial intelligence to compose or
complete an assignment as cheating, already treats unattributed
AI-composed work as plagiarism, and already places the burden on the
University to prove a violation by a preponderance of the evidence, with
written notice, an opportunity to inspect relevant documents, an advisor,
and an appeal; and,

WHEREAS, Divisional Regulation A-306 already provides that the instructor
determines the grade from the student's achievement, that suspected
plagiarism or cheating is reported for disciplinary proceedings, and that
the grade Deferred Report is assigned until those proceedings are
completed; and,

WHEREAS, neither the Interim 2026 Student Conduct Code nor Divisional
Regulation A-306 states whether a score, percentage, highlight, or other
output of a tool that classifies work as generated by artificial
intelligence is sufficient evidence of misconduct or a sufficient basis
for a grade penalty, and neither text states that a person must be able
to reach a different result from that output; and,

WHEREAS, a consequential decision for purposes of this resolution is a
finding of responsibility under the Student Conduct Code, a grade penalty
imposed because a student is suspected of unauthorized generative-AI use,
or a change in academic placement that relies on such an output; and,

WHEREAS, sample syllabus language published by UCLA Humanities Technology,
as recorded in the November 18, 2026 audit memorandum from a search
extraction of that page, invites an instructor to treat a "confirmed"
detector result as academic dishonesty without defining what confirmation
consists of or restating the Deferred Report sequence in Regulation
A-306, and that page is to be reopened before this resolution is
transmitted; and,

WHEREAS, the University of California Student Association on January 2,
2025, called for artificial-intelligence guidance in course syllabi, and
that resolution does not state an evidentiary rule, a notice rule, or a
rule that the student may use the existing review procedure to challenge
the output; therefore,

THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that it is the opinion of the Undergraduate
Students Association Council that a person makes any consequential
decision described in this resolution; that an artificial-intelligence
output may inform that person; that the person is able to reach a
different result; and that a score, percentage, highlight, or other
output of a generative-AI detection tool shall not be the sole basis for
a finding of responsibility under the UCLA Student Conduct Code and shall
not be the sole basis for a grade penalty imposed because a student is
suspected of unauthorized generative-AI use; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Council calls on the Los Angeles Division
of the Academic Senate to consider an amendment to Divisional Regulation
A-306, or a legislative ruling, providing that: when an instructor
suspects unauthorized generative-AI use, the instructor reports the
suspicion and assigns the grade Deferred Report until proceedings are
completed; before a finding, the student receives the course rule at
issue, the name of any detection tool, the date it was used, the output
relied upon, and a meaningful opportunity to respond, including with
drafts or other process evidence; a grade penalty for that suspicion is
imposed only after a finding that identifies the evidence other than the
detection output and states how the student's response was weighed; and
if the proceedings do not determine that the student engaged in the
suspected misconduct, the instructor grades the work without a penalty
based on that suspicion; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Council calls on the Chancellor, in
consultation with students, faculty, staff, and the Office of Student
Conduct, and subject to the review by the Office of the President that
the Student Conduct Code already requires before an amendment is adopted,
to consider adding to the Code a provision that: a generative-AI detection
output does not, by itself, meet the preponderance standard; the reviewer
states the other evidence in writing and addresses the student's
response; the student may use the Code's existing appeal to challenge a
finding that relied on that output; and student coursework is not uploaded
to a generative-AI detection service that the campus has not approved for
that purpose; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the existing conduct appeal and the existing
grade-grievance procedure are the accessible institutional processes for
this challenge; this resolution does not create a new board, a new fee,
or a new offense; and the reviewer in the existing procedure states in
writing the evidence other than the artificial-intelligence output; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Council calls on the Academic Senate and
the offices that publish teaching guidance to ask instructors to state,
before the relevant work is undertaken, whether generative-AI use is
permitted, restricted, or prohibited for that work, and to withdraw sample
language that treats an unspecified "confirmed" detector result as
academic dishonesty; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that when an instructor requires a student to use
an artificial-intelligence tool for an assignment and the student cannot
use that tool, the Council calls on the instructor's department to
provide a way to complete the same assignment without it; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the primary sponsor shall transmit this
resolution to the Chair of the Los Angeles Division of the Academic
Senate, the Office of Student Conduct, the Vice Chancellor for Student
Affairs, and the University of California Student Association, with a
request that UCSA consider a systemwide companion limited to the human
review, notice, challenge, and approved-tool rules in this resolution,
and not a restatement of its January 2, 2025 syllabus resolution; and,

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the Council expresses support for California
local educational agencies and charter schools that adopt, in their own
policies, the limit that an artificial-intelligence output is not the
sole basis for a grade penalty, a disciplinary decision, or a change in
placement, and directs no demand to any school district; and,

BE IT FINALLY RESOLVED, that this resolution expresses the opinion of the
current Council under the USA Bylaws, creates no new student-conduct
offense, finds no named student responsible or not responsible, and does
not adopt a numerical false-positive rate, a ban on detection tools, or a
requirement that a student disclose browsing history.
```
