**DRAFT. Not sent. Do not mail or email any text in this file until that specific send is approved.**

Research date: 2026-11-20. The sentences below are local proposals. No board in this file has adopted them.

## Who can actually introduce an amendment

A member of the public can write, and can speak on an item that is already on the agenda. The board adopts the policy that a member moves. The letter becomes part of the decision when a member moves the sentences and the board votes them into the policy.

Pasadena states an additional request path on https://www.pusd.us/about/board-of-education/board-meetings, opened November 20. A person may ask the Office of the Board of Education to place a matter of district business on an agenda. The written request, with backup, is due at least 12 days before the meeting. The Superintendent or designee decides whether the matter is district business and when it is agendized. That decision is the district's. It is not a right to a vote.

Tulare's November 4, 2026 agenda states the limit in the board's own notice. Members of the public may address an agenda item. The board cannot discuss or act on an item that is not on the agenda. A five-minute limit can be imposed.

## The gap these sentences fill

The California School Boards Association sample Board Policy 0441, read in the Visalia Unified first-reading packet of October 23, 2026, is nine principles that use "should," plus a direction that the superintendent keep AI use consistent with the technology plan, academic honesty, homework, assessment, and student-use policies. That sample does not say that a person makes the grade, the discipline, or the placement. It does not say what a detection score is worth. It does not say the student receives the tool, the date, the output, and a chance to respond. It does not point the student to the existing complaint procedure for this evidence. It does not bar an upload to a detector the district has not approved.

Visalia's packet is evidence of the sample. It is not evidence that Tulare or Pasadena pasted the sample unchanged. Each letter below is edited against the exhibit the board actually has, and any sentence already in that exhibit comes out of the letter before it is sent.

The sentences do not ban a tool, set a false-positive percentage, or require a student to produce browsing history.

## Shared operative sentences

Use "district" for Pasadena, Capistrano, and Palos Verdes. Use "County Office" for Tulare. Delete a paragraph that the exhibit already contains.

> A grade, a disciplinary decision, or a change in a student's placement or program is made by a person. An artificial-intelligence output may inform that person. That person is able to reach a different result. The output does not itself assign the grade, impose the discipline, or change the placement.
>
> A score, percentage, highlight, or other output of a tool that classifies student work as generated by artificial intelligence is not the sole basis for a grade penalty, a disciplinary decision, or a change in placement or program. Before any of those consequences, the student receives the applicable rule, the name of the tool, the date it was used, the output, and a meaningful chance to respond, including with drafts or other process evidence. The written decision states the evidence other than the output and states how the student's response was weighed.
>
> Student work is not submitted to a generative-AI detection service the [district / County Office] has not approved for that purpose.
>
> When the [district / County Office] requires a student to use an artificial-intelligence tool for an assignment and the student cannot use that tool, the [district / County Office] provides a way to complete the same assignment without it.
>
> A student or parent may use the [district / County Office]'s existing complaint, grievance, or appeal procedure to challenge a grade penalty, disciplinary decision, or placement change that relied on an artificial-intelligence output. The reviewer states in writing the evidence other than that output.

These five paragraphs are the human decision, the limit on the output, the notice and the chance to respond, the written record of the other evidence, the approved-tool boundary, the accessible way to finish the assignment, and the existing institutional process for a challenge.
