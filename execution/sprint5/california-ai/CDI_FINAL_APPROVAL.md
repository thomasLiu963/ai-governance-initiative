## Novelty

The three records below were checked against the statutes, the title 10 provisions, and the Department guidance named in this section. Each record asks for an artifact those sources do not already require.

| Proposed record | What already exists | What is still missing |

Bulletin 2022-5 (June 30, 2022) tells insurers to give those reasons and says the Department may examine algorithms and models. Title 10, section 2695.7, which the bulletin cites for claims, requires a written denial with its factual and legal bases. The utilization-review statute is limited to medical necessity and to the licensed professional. The letter leaves that determination where section 10123.135 put it. |

Title 10, sections 2648.4 and 2648.5 require model information in a complete rate application, including information showing that a model used for a property-and-casualty rate uses established concepts, data, equations, and principles. The letter proposes no ratio.

The 2022 workshop, REG-2022-00013, asked about third-party validation and the reasons for an adverse underwriting decision. No title 10 artificial-intelligence section was adopted after it. The May 5, 2025 guidance, “SB 1120:1,” interprets section 10123.135(j). Section 10123.135(j)(5) places that guidance outside the Administrative Procedure Act. ## What changed from the November 20 workshop draft

The earlier letter is `execution/sprint4/california-ai/CDI_SUBMISSION.md`. This letter keeps the three records and changes the trigger so an examiner can apply it.

- The record attaches when a covered output was a basis for the action. An unused score in the file does not trigger it.
- A covered output is a prediction, score, classification, or recommendation from a machine-based system. A factor the filed manual states in full, and applies by arithmetic the manual states, is outside the definition. A medical-necessity determination already made under section 10123.135 is outside it.
- The comparison names an outcome that is not the system’s output and is not a restatement of the action, and it names the population, the time window, and the result. There is still no passing score.
- A statement that no outcome is yet observable names one date. The insurer may replace that date once, before it arrives. Use after the replacement date, without the comparison, fails, and no further date may be recorded.
- The letter says in terms that sections 2648.4 and 2648.5 remain the rate-application path, and that a rate exhibit does not satisfy the consumer-file record.

## The agency action that would make this an accomplishment

The accomplishment is a title 10 regulation, filed with the Secretary of State, whose text requires the three records.

The path is the one Insurance Code section 790.10 describes, followed by the Administrative Procedure Act. Section 790.10 authorizes the Commissioner, from time to time as conditions warrant and after notice and public hearing, to promulgate reasonable rules necessary to administer the unfair-practices article. A later notice of proposed action, at least 45 days of comment, a final statement of reasons, Office of Administrative Law review, and filing with the Secretary of State are the remaining steps. This letter would have to be resubmitted in that comment period before section 11346.9 requires an answer.

By February 15, 2027, the external events this letter can produce are receipt of the December email and the December 15 workshop. The invitation gives no date for a notice of proposed action. A filed regulation is a later event.

Senate Bill 354’s new article operates July 1, 2028, and the new article’s rulemaking sentence is operative then. A regulation adopted before that date should cite the correction and adverse-underwriting sections that are operative on the date of the action, and it should be conformed when the new article operates.

## Letter

California Department of Insurance
REG-2026-00007
Written comment for the December 15, 2026 discussion workshop
Exploring the Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence in Insurance

Submitted to CDIRegulations@insurance.ca.gov

[Name]
[Email, only if it may be public]

I am commenting on the questions in the November 13, 2026 invitation. I understand that this invitation is not a Notice of Proposed Action, that the Department is not required to respond to this comment, and that this comment is carried into a later rulemaking file only if I resubmit it after a future notice. The text below is offered for that future notice.

The invitation asks what human review should occur, how an insurer should know that a system remains reliable after deployment, what an insurer needs in order to oversee a third-party system, and how a problem is identified and corrected. Bulletin 2022-5 already says the Department may examine algorithms and models, and it already identifies Insurance Code section 791.10 as the source of the specific reasons for an adverse underwriting decision. The 2022 workshop asked how insurers validate third-party tools and how they state those reasons. No regulation followed. The gap that remains is a record an examiner can check.

I propose three records. None of them sets a disparity ratio, a pass rate, or a protected-class threshold. Insurance Code sections 679.71, 790.03, 10140, and 1861.05, and the other statutes cited in Bulletin 2022-5, already prohibit unfair discrimination. A missing record is what an examiner can determine. A new percentage is not.

The records are documents about a system the insurer is already using as a basis for an adverse action. They are not a new rate exhibit and not a new consumer appeal. Title 10, sections 2648.4 and 2648.5, already require model information in a complete rate application. This comment does not amend those sections. A rate-application exhibit does not satisfy the consumer-file records below.

Insurance Code section 790.10 authorizes the Commissioner, after notice and public hearing, to promulgate reasonable rules necessary to administer the unfair-practices article. That is the proceeding in which this text belongs.

### Covered output

A covered output is a prediction, score, classification, or recommendation produced by a machine-based system and used as a basis for an action described below.

A covered output does not include a factor that the insurer’s filed rate manual, or its complete rate application, states in full and applies by the arithmetic that manual or application states. A covered output does not include a determination of medical necessity that is made, and documented, by a licensed physician or a licensed health care professional competent to evaluate the clinical issues, as Insurance Code section 10123.135 requires. That statute, and the guidance issued under section 10123.135(j)(5), stay where they are.

### 1. Human-review record

This responds to the invitation’s questions on human review, human judgment, and intervention.

When a covered output is a basis for an adverse underwriting decision, as defined in Insurance Code section 791.02, or for a denial of a claim in whole or in part, the insurer keeps a record in the file for that action stating all of the following:

1. The name of the person who reviewed the action before the insurer communicated it.
2. That the person reviewed the covered output and the consumer-specific data the system used to produce it.
3. That the person had authority to change the action.
4. Whether the person adopted the output, changed the action, or did not use the output as a basis for the action.

An examiner compares the file with the record. A file that uses a covered output as a basis for the action, and contains none of the four items, fails. A file that contains the four items can be checked against the action that was communicated. The record does not require the person to be a particular vendor’s employee, and it does not require the insurer to disclose model weights.

The record does not change the notice Insurance Code section 791.10 already requires after an adverse underwriting decision. It does not change the claim communications Bulletin 2022-5 identifies under Insurance Code section 790.03 and title 10, section 2695.7.

### 2. Version record

This responds to the invitation’s questions on reliability, monitoring, and what insurers should know before deployment. The invitation states that a system may behave differently after deployment than it did during development.

Before an insurer uses a version of a system as a basis for an action described in item 1, the insurer keeps a record for that version stating all of the following:

1. The version identifier.
2. The action the version is permitted to support.
3. The data elements the version uses.
4. The date of the evaluation and the name of the person who accepted the version for that use.
5. Either (A) or (B).

(A) A comparison, performed on data not used to build that version, of the version’s output to an outcome the insurer can observe. The outcome is not the output itself and is not a restatement of the action. The record names the outcome, the population, the time window, the method, and the result.

(B) A statement that no such outcome is yet observable, and one date. If the insurer uses the version as a basis for an action described in item 1 after that date, and the record does not yet contain the comparison in (A), the file fails. Before that date, the insurer may replace it once with one later date. Use after the replacement date without the comparison in (A) fails. The insurer may not record a further date. A statement with no date fails.

The insurer makes a new record before it uses a changed version as a basis for an action described in item 1.

An examiner fails the file if the version used for the action has no record, or if the record is dated after the action. The examiner can read the result of the comparison. The regulation would not declare that result pass or fail, and it would not set a number. If a later notice proposes a numeric standard, that standard needs a defined population, a defined outcome, and a stated comparison. This comment does not supply those, because the invitation does not contain them.

Section 10123.135(j)(1)(I) continues to require periodic review of a utilization-review tool. This version record does not add a second review of a medical-necessity determination, and it does not replace that periodic review.

### 3. Third-party systems, and a new review after a correction

This responds to the invitation’s questions on third-party systems and on identifying, investigating, and correcting problems.

If a person other than the insurer supplies the system, the insurer still keeps the records in items 1 and 2. Those records are in the insurer’s possession. A contract term that places responsibility for the system on the supplier is not a substitute. The insurer may obtain the version identifier, the data elements, and the comparison or the dated statement from the supplier. The insurer does not need the supplier’s model weights or source code. If the supplier does not provide the version identifier, the data elements, and either the comparison or the dated statement, the insurer does not use that version as a basis for an action described in item 1.

An examiner looks in the insurer’s file.

Insurance Code section 791.10 already requires, after an adverse underwriting decision, the specific reasons and the specific items of personal and privileged information that support those reasons, subject to the exceptions in that section, including the exception for privileged information when the insurer has a reasonable suspicion of criminal activity, fraud, material misrepresentation, or material nondisclosure. Section 791.09 already requires the insurer, within 30 business days of a written request, to correct, amend, or delete the disputed recorded personal information, or to refuse in writing and state the reasons. Neither section requires the insurer to review the adverse underwriting decision again after a correction of an item that supported it.

Where the insurer corrects, amends, or deletes an item under section 791.09, that item is one of the specific items that supported an adverse underwriting decision under section 791.10, and a covered output was a basis for that decision, the insurer, before leaving the decision in effect, either records a new review under item 1 that uses the corrected item, or records that the decision does not depend on the corrected item. The insurer furnishes to the consumer only what section 791.10 already requires it to furnish. This paragraph does not apply to an item the insurer is permitted to withhold under section 791.10.

Senate Bill 354, Chapter 481, Statutes of 2026, revises this article operative July 1, 2028. The chaptered section 791.09 again requires correction, amendment, or deletion, and it authorizes regulations to implement that section. It does not require the new review described in this item. Any regulation should use the correction and adverse-underwriting sections that are operative on the date of the action, and it should be conformed when the new article operates. This comment does not ask the Department to apply the 2028 article before its operative date.

### What I am not asking

I am not asking for a numeric fairness test, a prohibition on a class of models, a new consumer complaint bureau, an amendment of section 10123.135, or an amendment of title 10, sections 2648.4 or 2648.5. I am not asking the Department to treat this workshop comment as a comment on proposed regulatory text. If the Department issues a notice of proposed action, I will resubmit the portion of this comment that still applies to the noticed text.

Authority for a future regulation on these records: Insurance Code section 790.10, in a proceeding that also respects sections 791.02, 791.09, 791.10, and 10123.135.
