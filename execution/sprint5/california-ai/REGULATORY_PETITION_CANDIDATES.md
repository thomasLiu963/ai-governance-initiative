# Regulatory petition candidates

Prepared 2026-11-20. No petition in this file has been mailed or emailed.

A Government Code section 11340.6 petition asks an agency to adopt, amend, or repeal a regulation. It states the substance of the requested regulation, the reason, and the agency’s authority. Section 11340.7 requires written notice of receipt and, within 30 days, a written denial stating the reasons or a schedule for a public hearing under Article 5. The agency sends the written decision to the Office of Administrative Law for the Notice Register. Section 11340.85(b)(5) requires the agency to have expressly agreed before a petition may be sent only by email. The petitions below are mailed.

The institutional act section 11340.7 can produce is that writing. A denial with reasons is a decision. It is not adoption of the safeguard. A hearing date starts the Administrative Procedure Act. Adoption is a later regulation, approved by the Office of Administrative Law and filed with the Secretary of State, whose text contains the requested duty. February 15, 2027, can include the written decision for a petition received in December. It does not include a filed amendment unless the agency both schedules a hearing and completes the article 5 proceeding, which these records do not establish.

Section 11342.2 limits a regulation to what is consistent with the statute and reasonably necessary to effectuate its purpose. A petition that asks an agency to do what the statute does not authorize will produce a denial. That denial is not a clarification of the law and is not an accomplishment.

## 1. Civil Rights Council — record when a system is a basis for hiring or promotion

DRAFT — NOT MAILED. Mail only if an approval in `ACTION_QUEUE.md` names this petition. On the day of signing, re-read title 2, sections 11008(h), 11008(i), 11008.1(a), 11009(f), and 11013(c). If section 11009(f) already requires this record, do not mail the letter.

This petition is separate from the citation petition. Mail them in separate envelopes. A denial of this duty should not decide the citation.

The Council’s final statement of reasons, June 2025, declined a request to require human oversight of every final employment decision. The response was that the request was broader than section 11017.1, which implements the Fair Chance Act, Government Code section 12952. The response did not amend section 11009(f) or section 11013(c). This petition is narrower than that request. It does not cover a disciplinary or termination decision. Labor Code section 1522, operative July 1, 2027, already prohibits sole reliance and requires human corroboration for those decisions, and it gives the Labor Commissioner no rulemaking authority. Labor Code section 1526.3 says an employer who gives the notice that part requires is not also required to give a substantially similar automated-decision notice under another state law. This petition asks for a record in the file the employer already keeps. It asks for no new notice, and it asks for nothing about discipline or termination.

What the current regulations do. Section 11008.1(a) defines an automated-decision system as a computational process that makes a decision or facilitates human decision making regarding an employment benefit. The final text and the Westlaw display still cite “section 11008(i)” for that term. Section 11008(h) defines “Employment Benefit.” Section 11008(i) defines “Employment Practice.” Section 11009(f) makes it unlawful to use an automated-decision system or selection criteria that discriminates on a basis the Act protects, and it makes anti-bias testing relevant to the claim and to the defense. Section 11013(c) requires the employer to preserve automated-decision system data for four years. It does not say the file must show that a person reviewed the data that produced the output used for the particular decision, or that the person had authority to reach a different decision.

The necessity argument, which the Council can reject, is that section 11009(f) cannot be examined for a particular hiring or promotion decision if the retained data do not show whether any person reviewed the data behind the output. A system that “makes a decision,” which the definition allows, and a person who had authority to depart from it, are different facts. The record makes that fact visible. It does not ban the system, and it does not require the person to reject the output.

I am an interested person. I petition the Civil Rights Council to amend title 2, California Code of Regulations, section 11013, to add one record.

1. Substance of the amendment requested

Add subsection (d) to section 11013:

> (d) When an employer or other covered entity uses an output of an automated-decision system, as defined in section 11008.1, as a basis for denying or limiting a hiring decision or a promotion, the records preserved under subsection (c) include a record stating all of the following:
>
> (1) The name of the person who reviewed the decision before it was communicated.
>
> (2) That the person reviewed the output and the applicant-specific or employee-specific data the system used to produce the output.
>
> (3) That the person had authority to reach a different decision.
>
> (4) Whether the person adopted the output, changed the decision, or did not use the output as a basis for the decision.
>
> This subsection does not apply to a disciplinary decision or a termination decision.

No other section is requested to be changed. Section 11008.1 is not requested to be changed. This petition does not ask the Council to require human oversight of every employment decision, to ban an automated-decision system, or to create a new notice to the applicant or employee.

2. Reason for the request

Section 11008.1(a) defines an automated-decision system as a computational process that makes a decision or facilitates human decision making regarding an employment benefit. Section 11009(f) makes discriminatory use of such a system unlawful and makes the existence, quality, and results of anti-bias testing relevant. Section 11013(c) requires preservation of automated-decision system data for four years and does not identify a field showing whether a person reviewed the data behind the output used for the decision.

Labor Code section 1522, added by Senate Bill 947, Chapter 859, Statutes of 2026, and operative July 1, 2027, prohibits an employer from relying solely on an automated decision system for a disciplinary or termination decision, and it requires human corroboration when the employer primarily relies on the output. The part contains no grant of authority to adopt implementing regulations. It does not apply to a hiring decision or a promotion. This subsection covers those two decisions and stops where section 1522 starts. It is a record, not a notice, so it is not a second notice under Labor Code section 1526.3.

The Council’s 2025 final statement of reasons declined to require human oversight of all final employment decisions inside the Fair Chance Act regulation. This request is limited to the file already required by section 11013, and to hiring and promotion.

If the Council concludes that section 11009(f) does not make this record reasonably necessary, please deny the petition in writing and say so.

3. Authority to take the action

Government Code section 12935(a) authorizes the Council to adopt, promulgate, amend, and rescind regulations that interpret, implement, and apply the Fair Employment and Housing Act, including Government Code section 12940. Sections 11008.1, 11009, and 11013 were adopted under that authority. Government Code section 11340.6 authorizes an interested person to petition for the amendment. Government Code section 11342.2 requires the regulation to be consistent with the statute and reasonably necessary to effectuate its purpose.

Please respond as Government Code section 11340.7 requires, including written notice of receipt and, within 30 days, a written denial stating the reasons or scheduling of the matter for public hearing under Article 5 (commencing with section 11346). Please send the written decision to the Office of Administrative Law for publication in the California Regulatory Notice Register as section 11340.7(d) requires.

## 3. Candidates that do not support a petition

### Senate Bill 947

Chapter 859, Statutes of 2026, was read in the chaptered text published October 1, 2026. Part 5.5.5, sections 1520 through 1526.7, becomes operative July 1, 2027. Section 1522(b) prohibits sole reliance on an automated decision system for a disciplinary or termination decision and requires human corroboration when reliance is primary. Section 1524 requires the post-use notice. Section 1526.1 assigns enforcement to the Labor Commissioner, through Labor Code sections 98.3, 98.7, 98.74, and 1197.1, and to a public prosecutor. The part contains no sentence authorizing implementing regulations. Section 1526.1(c) uses “disciplinary or deactivation decision” in the burden-shifting sentence, while section 1522 uses “disciplinary or termination decision.” That difference is in the statute. A regulation cannot amend it. A petition asking the Labor Commissioner or the Department of Industrial Relations to define “primarily,” to define termination, or to copy section 1522 into a regulation asks for an action this part does not authorize. The duties operate on July 1, 2027 without a regulation. No petition is drafted.

### Government artificial intelligence

Government Code section 11549.63(h), added by Senate Bill 896, says state agencies should consider rulemaking and should emphasize whether existing regulations apply to generative artificial intelligence. “Should” is not a mandate and is not a grant that makes one criterion reasonably necessary. The State Administrative Manual, section 4986.12, already says generative-artificial-intelligence output used for decision making must be supplemented with human verification. That manual section is not a regulation adopted under the Administrative Procedure Act, and converting it into one is a program the statute does not require this project to open. The Department of Technology’s SIMM 5305-F risk assessment already asks how system owners will accept, reject, and correct outputs. No notice of proposed action was found that asks for a further oversight sentence. No petition is drafted.

Government Code section 8898.1 and Executive Order N-9-26 are the Government Operations Agency criteria. The order’s posting date is May 1, 2027. No draft criteria and no comment docket were found. A petition now asks the Agency to open a rulemaking for one criterion before the consultation section 8898.2 describes. The section 11340.7 writing would be a denial or a hearing date. The held reproducibility criterion remains in `execution/sprint4/california-ai/GOVOPS_FEASIBILITY.md`, for use if the Agency opens a written process. No petition is drafted.
