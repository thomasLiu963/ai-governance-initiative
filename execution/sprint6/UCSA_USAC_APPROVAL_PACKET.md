**Who may introduce.** Bylaws, the resolution subsection: “Any members of board may introduce a resolution.” An ordinary UCLA undergraduate is not a board member. The undergraduate may submit the Agenda Item Request form. The form says it is used for all agenda requests, caps an item at 25 minutes, and points questions to chair@ucsa.org. It does not say that a request from a non-member is placed on the agenda. The person who can introduce this resolution is Cecilia Choy.

**Vote.** At a board meeting, passage is a majority of the Board members present, with a quorum of at least 51 percent of voting members. The resolution must be shared with all board members at least two business days before the vote. The Board Chair gives written notice and the agenda at least five business days before a regular meeting. A bylaw amendment is a different filing: the form says the amendment text must also be emailed to the full board at least one week before the meeting. This resolution does not amend the bylaws.

**Meetings inside the horizon.** The event-dates document lists a virtual board meeting on **Saturday, January 9, 2027**, and a hybrid meeting on **February 20–21, 2027**, at UC Santa Barbara. The July board notes record a motion to approve the 2026–27 calendar and a proposed move of the February meeting to those dates. The notes do not record the vote tally. On the agendas page, the January 2027 and February 2027 lines have no document link. February 20–21 is after February 15. The only in-horizon meeting to aim at is January 9, and only after Choy confirms that date survived the July vote.

For a January 9 vote, the bylaw clocks are: agenda notice by Monday, January 4, 2027, and circulation of the resolution by Wednesday, January 6, 2027. Those dates count business days backward from Saturday, January 9, and they assume the meeting is held that day.

**What passage is.** A passed resolution updates UCSA’s platform and asks the systemwide Academic Senate and the Office of the President to consider text. It does not amend a campus conduct code, a divisional regulation, or a systemwide policy. The minutes naming the resolution and the vote are the UCSA result. A later Senate or Office of the President adoption is a separate result.

## How this differs from the January 2, 2025 resolution

The adopted resolution is titled “Resolution for Support on Strong Recommendation for AI Guidance in Syllabi.” The index title is different. Its resolved clauses ask the Academic Senate for syllabus language on permitted AI use, a student representative on AI academic committees, faculty training, a feedback mechanism, an annual campus report, and a toolkit. They do not say that a detection output is not the sole basis for a conduct finding or a grade penalty, that the student receives the tool, the date, the output, and a chance to respond, that a person can reach a different result, or that the existing appeal is the way to challenge the output. This resolution does not restate the syllabus mandate.

## Resolution for Choy to introduce

Use the template’s platform sentence. Leave the author line blank on any copy that would be posted as the board document until Choy decides whether to list the drafter. The local authorship note below is the record either way.

```
Human Review When an Artificial-Intelligence Output Is Used in a Consequential Student Decision
January 9, 2027

Author(s): Cecilia Choy, UCLA External Vice President, if she agrees to introduce it

WHEREAS, on January 2, 2025, this Board called for artificial-intelligence guidance in course syllabi, and that resolution does not state what a detection output is worth in a conduct finding, a grade penalty, or a change in academic placement; and

WHEREAS, a consequential decision in this resolution is a finding of academic misconduct, a grade penalty imposed because a student is suspected of unauthorized generative-AI use, or a change in academic placement that relies on a score, percentage, highlight, or other output of a tool that classifies work as generated by artificial intelligence; and

THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, the official platform of the UC Student Association shall be updated to represent the values expressed in this resolution; and,

THEREFORE BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that it is the position of this Board that a person makes any such consequential decision, that an artificial-intelligence output may inform that person, that the person is able to reach a different result, and that the output is not the sole basis for the decision; and,

THEREFORE BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that this Board calls on the systemwide Academic Senate and the Office of the President to consider, for each undergraduate campus, a rule that before any such decision the student receives the applicable rule, the name of the tool, the date it was used, the output, and a meaningful chance to respond, including with drafts or other process evidence; that the written decision states the evidence other than the output and how the student’s response was weighed; that the student may use the campus’s existing conduct appeal or grade grievance to challenge the decision; and that coursework is not uploaded to a detection service the campus has not approved for that purpose; and,

THEREFORE BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that this resolution does not amend a campus code or a Senate regulation, does not decide any student’s case, and does not restate the January 2, 2025 syllabus resolution.
```

## How to document the student’s authorship

Bylaws Article VI, Section A.5.e: “No author or sponsor’s name shall be included in any published resolution.” The Resolution Guide applies that rule to a paid advertisement of the resolution. The agenda packet is a different document: the bylaws require the resolution’s text in the packet, and the guide’s own template has sponsor lines. Posted resolution PDFs on the USAC site have included sponsor names. This plan does not put the student’s name into an advertisement or into a resolution the Council publishes for general distribution.

1. Before the first sponsor email, save this sentence in the project, dated and signed by the student: “I drafted the resolution in `execution/sprint5/institutional-policy/UCLA_APPROVAL_PACKET.md`. I am asking named Council members to sponsor it. I understand that a published resolution omits author and sponsor names.”
2. A sponsor line is filled only after that officer replies, in writing, “I will sponsor the resolution you drafted.” Save the reply in the project. That reply is the authorship record. It is not attached to the Council packet and it is not posted.
3. The packet filed with the Internal Vice President names the three sponsors, because the bylaws require three sponsors and the packet is the Council’s working text. It does not name the undergraduate drafter.
4. After passage, the primary sponsor’s transmission to the Academic Senate, the Office of Student Conduct, the Vice Chancellor for Student Affairs, and UCSA may include one sentence: “An undergraduate drafted this resolution, and the Council adopted it.” That sentence is the public attribution that does not violate the publication rule.
5. Do not place the student’s name, or the sponsors’ names, on a paid notice, a social-media image of the resolution, or a later PDF issued as the published resolution.

The USAC result remains the posted minutes and the packet. The local email file is evidence of who drafted it. It is not evidence that the Council adopted the resolution.
