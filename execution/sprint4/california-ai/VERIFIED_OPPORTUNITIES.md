## What the words mean

| Word | What has happened |
| --- | --- |
| Submission | The agency received the writing at the address and by the time its own notice states. |
| Acknowledgment | The agency wrote back that it received the writing. A silent inbox is not an acknowledgment. |
| Consultation | The agency invited the person into a process its statute or notice describes, and the person took part. |
| Formal consideration | A noticed rulemaking exists, and Government Code section 11346.9 requires the final statement of reasons to answer a timely comment. Placement in a file is the notice’s ordinary duty. It is not acceptance. |
| Accepted proposal | The agency’s later official text uses the proposal’s operative requirement. |
| Published guidance | The agency posts a bulletin, guidance, or report that states the requirement. A workshop invitation is not that document. |
| Adopted regulation | The Office of Administrative Law has approved the text, or the statute exempts it, and the text is filed with the Secretary of State. |

A workshop comment can be a submission and a California Public Records Act record. The Department of Insurance invitation says it is not, by itself, formal consideration.

- Workshop: December 15, 2026, 10:00 a.m. Pacific time, virtual, until everyone present who wishes to speak has spoken, or noon, whichever is earlier. Registration: https://us06web.zoom.us/webinar/register/WN_0suMNeCLRPiwCWW8U87uKQ
- Written comments: CDIRegulations@insurance.ca.gov by December 12, 2026. Supporting materials may be attached.
- Substantive contact: Kathryn Taras, Chief, Office of the Special Counsel, (916) 610-2034. Logistics: Randi Bovard, (916) 610-0964. Same email.
- Public record: Government Code section 7920.000 and following. Name, address, telephone, and email can be released.
- The invitation’s discussion questions cover current uses, consumer protection and human review, governance and accountability, data and third-party systems, transparency, and future standards. The workshop will not focus on regulatory text. Participants may still propose it.
- Press release the same day, https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0100-press-releases/2026/release042-2026.cfm. The Department is not adopting a regulation that day. Any later formal proposal would have its own comment period.

Prior Department work, cited by the invitation and read for this pass:

- Bulletin 2022-5, June 30, 2022. A reminder of existing duties, including Insurance Code sections 790.03 and 791.10 and title 10, section 2695.7. The Department says it may examine algorithms and models in a market-conduct or special-investigation examination. The bulletin was not adopted under the Administrative Procedure Act.
- Prenotice workshop REG-2022-00013, invitation August 23, 2022, workshop September 21, 2022. Same disclaimer: not a notice of proposed action, no duty to respond, comments carry forward only if resubmitted. Questions included third-party validation, bias testing, and the specific reasons for an adverse underwriting decision. No title 10 artificial-intelligence section was adopted after that workshop.
- The 2023 automated-decision survey is named in the 2026 invitation. This pass did not retrieve a published tabulation of that survey.
- Insurance Code section 10123.135(j) requires a licensed physician or other licensed professional to make a medical-necessity determination, and paragraph (5) authorizes Department guidance that is not subject to the Administrative Procedure Act. The May 5, 2025 utilization-review guidance sits under that paragraph. The comment leaves that determination where the statute put it.
- Insurance Code section 790.10, read 2026-11-20: the commissioner shall, from time to time as conditions warrant, after notice and public hearing, promulgate reasonable rules and regulations necessary to administer the unfair-practices article.
- Insurance Code sections 791.02, 791.09, and 791.10, current article, read 2026-11-20. An adverse underwriting decision already requires specific reasons and the specific items of information that support them, with stated exceptions. A written request to correct recorded personal information must be granted or refused, with reasons, within 30 business days. The correction section does not require the insurer to review the underwriting decision again after the correction.
- Senate Bill 354, Chapter 481, Statutes of 2026. New Article 6.6 becomes operative July 1, 2028. The current article becomes inoperative that day and is repealed January 1, 2029. The new article again provides access, correction, and reasons for adverse underwriting decisions, and it authorizes commissioner regulations. The comment uses the current sections and asks that any later regulation be conformed when the new article operates. It does not apply the 2028 article early.

The three proposals, and the path from this comment to a title 10 section, are in `CDI_SUBMISSION.md`.
