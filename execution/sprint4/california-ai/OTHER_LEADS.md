## Senate Bill 947 — workplace automated-decision systems

Chapter 859, Statutes of 2026. Approved and filed September 30, 2026. Adds Labor Code Part 5.5.5, sections 1520 through 1526.7. Section 1526.7: the part becomes operative July 1, 2027.

The part is enforced by the Labor Commissioner, through the procedures in Labor Code sections 98.3, 98.7, 98.74, and 1197.1, and by a public prosecutor under sections 180 and following. Section 1526.1. The civil penalty is $500 per violation. Section 1526.1(e). The Civil Rights Department is not the enforcing agency. The Civil Rights Council’s title 2 employment regulations, including section 11008.1, are a different instrument. Section 1526.4 keeps a covered business subject to automated-decision regulations the Privacy Protection Agency adopts under the California Consumer Privacy Act. It does not move enforcement of this part to that Agency.

What the statute already requires, so a petition would not be adding it:

- Section 1522(b)(1): an employer shall not rely solely on an automated decision system when making a disciplinary or termination decision.
- Section 1522(b)(2): if the employer primarily relies on an automated-decision output for that decision, the employer shall direct a human to corroborate it using the data that was collected or used to produce the output, or other relevant information. The section lists supervisory evaluations, personnel files, work product, peer reviews, and witness interviews, including relevant online customer reviews.
- Section 1522(c): if the employer cannot corroborate the output, or the human reviewer concludes that the output is inaccurate, incomplete, or misleading, the employer shall not use the output for the disciplinary or termination decision.
- Section 1522(d): the employee may request, and the employer shall provide, a meaningful, objective description of the employee’s own data used by the system, with other people’s personal information anonymized.
- Section 1524: a written post-use notice, at the time the employer informs the employee, in plain language, as a separate communication, in the language used for routine communications. The notice states that the employer primarily relied on the system, that a human reviewed the decision and corroborated the output, how to reach a human, and that retaliation is prohibited.
- Section 1526: retaliation for using the part is itself prohibited.

The database’s proposed regulation, defining “release from employment” and declaring an unreviewed firing a violation, does not add a duty the statute left out. The operative words are “disciplinary or termination decision.” Section 1526.1(c) uses “disciplinary or deactivation decision” in the burden-shifting sentence. That word differs from section 1522. The difference is in the statute. A regulation cannot amend it. The Legislature is the body that can.

The part contains no grant of authority to adopt implementing regulations. Labor Code section 1699, which authorizes Labor Commissioner regulations, applies to a different chapter. Government Code section 11340.6 requires the petition to refer to the agency’s authority to take the action requested. Government Code section 11342.2 says a regulation is valid only if it is consistent with the statute and reasonably necessary to effectuate its purpose. A petition asking the Department of Industrial Relations to define termination, to require system logs, or to copy section 1522 into a regulation asks for an action this part does not authorize. The duties operate on July 1, 2027 without a regulation.

A petition is therefore not supported. Filing one in order to receive a written denial would produce a denial, which is not an accepted safeguard and is not a truthful account of having clarified the law. No petition is drafted.

The database’s admissions sentence, about an unpaid internship program and a fictitious example, is not a description of this statute. It is not used.

## California Department of Education — model artificial-intelligence policy

Senate Bill 1288 (Becker, 2024) required a model policy. The Department’s page, https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/pl/aipolicy.asp, states that the policy was developed with the Artificial Intelligence Working Group, that local educational agencies and charter schools may adapt it, and that compliance with the document is not mandatory. The page cites Education Code section 33308.5.

The indexed text of the policy already addresses several of the safeguards a new comment would be tempted to invent. Artificial-intelligence detection software should not be the sole basis for discipline or a grade penalty. Concerns about misuse rely on educator evaluation of student work, an alternative assessment, and documented evidence of authorship, with a way for the student and family to rebut the claim. Educators decide when a particular assignment may use the tools, and they say so. Students and educators do not enter education records into a tool unless the local policy authorizes it. Tools should prohibit use of student data to train public models or for advertising. Grading or feedback that uses the tools requires notification and consent. Those sentences are guidance. They are not title 5 regulations.

The working-group page, https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/pl/aiineducationworkgroup.asp, was returned by search on 2026-11-20. A direct fetch the same day returned 403, so this paragraph rests on the indexed page and should be rechecked in a browser before anyone relies on the calendar. The indexed page says the group was convened under Senate Bill 1288, that meetings are subject to the Bagley-Keene Open Meeting Act, and that agendas included a public-comment item. The listed meetings run through August 17, 2026. No later meeting was in that index.

The legislative-report page, https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/pl/ailegreport.asp, says the report is not available and is due December 2026. The page’s last-reviewed line is June 25, 2026.

There is no open notice of proposed action and no published draft of the December report on which a comment could be filed. An email to the professional-learning address would be an unsolicited suggestion about a nonbinding document. It would not be consultation, formal consideration, or an amendment of the model policy. If the Department later posts a draft report and invites comment, or notices another public meeting, the question can be reopened against that notice. Until then, no letter is drafted.

Education Code section 33308.5 is also why the guidance is not treated as an underground regulation. The page says compliance is not mandatory.

## CalOES — frontier artificial-intelligence incident reporting

The reporting statute is Senate Bill 53, Chapter 138, Statutes of 2025, the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act. It is not Senate Bill 354. Senate Bill 354, Chapter 481, Statutes of 2026, is the Insurance Information and Privacy Protection Act.

Business and Professions Code section 22757.13(a), as returned from the official display, requires the Office of Emergency Services to establish a mechanism a frontier developer or a member of the public can use to report a critical safety incident. The report includes the date, the reasons the incident qualifies, a short and plain statement, and whether it was associated with internal use of a frontier model. Section 22757.12(d) is a different duty: a large frontier developer transmits a summary of assessments of catastrophic risk from internal use, every three months or on another written schedule. The database cited 22757.12(d) for the public mechanism. The public mechanism is section 22757.13.

Section 22757.13(d): the Office reviews critical-safety-incident reports from frontier developers and may review reports from members of the public. Section 22757.13(f): a critical-safety-incident report, a catastrophic-risk summary, and a covered-employee report are exempt from the California Public Records Act. Section 22757.13(g): beginning January 1, 2027, and annually after that, the Office produces an anonymized and aggregated report for the Legislature and the Governor, omitting information that would compromise trade secrets, cybersecurity, public safety, or national security.

The Office’s page describes the portal for those statutory reports and a worksheet of the portal’s fields:

https://www.caloes.ca.gov/office-of-the-director/operations/homeland-security/california-cybersecurity-integration-center/transparency-in-frontier-ai-act-reporting/

The page, as returned in search, is an intake for incidents and for developers’ assessment summaries. It is not a docket for proposed changes to the reporting framework. This pass did not open the portal and did not file anything.

A test report is not a technical contribution. The mechanism is for a critical safety incident. Filing a report that is not one would not be independently verifiable, because section 22757.13(f) exempts the report from public disclosure. The Office’s annual aggregate, beginning January 1, 2027, is not a response to a suggested field change. No regulation-comment notice for this framework was in the November registers read for this pass.

No comment, no suggested schema, and no test report is drafted.
