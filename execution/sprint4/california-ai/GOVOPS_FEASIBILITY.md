## The consultation the statute actually creates

Government Code section 8898.2(a) says the Agency shall consult as appropriate with auditors, independent verification organizations, academic institutions, developers, deployers, consumer, labor, and civil-society organizations, other agencies, standards bodies, and other stakeholders the Agency determines to be relevant. “As appropriate” and “as determined by the agency” are the Agency’s choices.

Section 8898.2(b)(1) says the Agency shall convene working groups to solicit stakeholder input on standards, procedures, requirements, and criteria. Working groups shall include, and are not limited to, engineers from AI companies that are competitors and AI safety experts. The statute does not say that a person may apply for a seat, and it does not say that an unsolicited memorandum is a working-group submission. Section 8898.2(b)(2) requires a report to the Legislature under Government Code section 9795. No such report was found in this pass. That absence is the state of the search. It is not a finding that the Agency has failed a deadline. The statutory date for the criteria themselves is January 1, 2028, accelerated by the order to May 1, 2027.

A student who emails GovOps has sent mail. The student has not joined a working group, has not been consulted under section 8898.2, and has no statutory right to a written answer.

## A petition is a poor fit

Assembly Bill 1405, section 11549.85, says the Agency may adopt regulations reasonably necessary to effectuate that chapter, under the Administrative Procedure Act. Senate Bill 813 tells the Agency to develop and post criteria. It does not say those criteria must be a regulation. Government Code section 11340.6 would let an interested person petition for a regulation if no statute restricts the petition or prescribes another procedure. This pass found no statute that confines section 8898.1 petitions to a designated group.

A petition filed now would ask the Agency to open a rulemaking for one criterion before it has posted the criteria section 8898.1 tells it to develop through consultation. The written act section 11340.7 can produce within 30 days is a denial with reasons, or a decision to schedule a hearing. A denial is not acceptance of the criterion. A hearing schedule is not a posted standard. The order’s posting date, May 1, 2027, is after February 15, 2027. This file does not include a petition, and none should be sent in order to obtain a denial.

## Held criterion

Use this only if GovOps publishes draft section 8898.1 criteria, or opens a written-comment process for those criteria, and a later approval names that process.

The statute already requires the applicant to submit the benchmarks, technologies, metrics, and methodologies it proposes to use. Section 8898.1(a)(C). The Agency must consider whether the organization can assess risks and identify the metrics and methodologies that form the basis of the assessment. Section 8898.1(c)(2)(A). The Agency must also consider conflicts of interest and independence, including a ban on payment terms that condition payment, or the amount of payment, on the result. Section 8898.1(c)(2)(C) and (D).

Assembly Bill 1405, section 11549.80(f), already states independence rules for a registered auditor: no audit where a financial, business, or employment interest would reasonably be expected to impair independence; reasonable compensation is not itself that interest; no evaluation of the auditor’s own prior design or operation; no employment negotiation with the auditee during the audit; a 12-month cooling-off period for a person who held material responsibility for the subject matter; independent judgment; competent personnel; and any additional professional standards the Agency adopts.

The statute does not say what record makes a methodology reproducible. The November 16 report does not supply that record. The missing piece is an artifact a second person can use.

Proposed designation criterion, for each methodology the applicant proposes to use:

> The application identifies, for each methodology: (1) the recorded inputs; (2) the procedure; (3) the output; (4) the record a second person, who did not produce the original output, would use to determine whether the same procedure applied to the same recorded inputs produces the same output or, where the procedure is not deterministic, the recorded control and the recorded range of outputs; and (5) the name of the person who accepted the conclusion and a statement that the person had authority to reject it. An application that names a metric and does not identify that record does not meet this criterion.

The criterion does not set a risk threshold. It does not restate the independence rules the statutes already contain. It does not ask for a seat on a working group. An examiner can apply it to the application file: the five items are there, or they are not.

If that criterion later appears in the posted section 8898.1 criteria, the truthful sentence is that the Government Operations Agency’s posted designation criteria require an independent verification organization to identify the record a second person would use to repeat each methodology. A letter in a drawer is not that sentence. The posting date under the order is May 1, 2027, which is outside the February 15 checkpoint.
