## Cover

**To:** CDIRegulations@insurance.ca.gov

**Subject:** REG-2026-00007 — Written comment, Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence in Insurance

**Deadline:** December 12, 2026. The invitation states that date. It does not state an hour.

**What this is.** A pre-rulemaking comment. Invitation page 6 states that the invitation is not a Notice of Proposed Action, that the Department is not required to respond, and that comments received for the workshop may not be included in a later rulemaking record. They are considered in a later proceeding only if they are resubmitted after a future notice. The same page states that the Commissioner will consider workshop comments while contemplating regulatory changes that may be proposed in a future notice.

**What a sent email is.** A submission, and a public record under Government Code section 7920.000 and following. It is not formal consideration under Government Code section 11346.9, not an accepted proposal, not guidance, and not a regulation.

**How the three records could become a regulation.**

1. The Commissioner decides to propose text. Insurance Code section 790.10 authorizes reasonable rules, after notice and public hearing, as necessary to administer the unfair-practices article. The Administrative Procedure Act then applies: a notice of proposed action, at least 45 days of comment, a final statement of reasons, Office of Administrative Law review, and filing with the Secretary of State. Title 10 is where a Department regulation is published.
2. This letter would have to be resubmitted in that comment period before the Department has a duty to answer it.
3. Bulletin 2022-5 and the May 5, 2025 utilization-review guidance are not that path. Insurance Code section 10123.135(j)(5) places guidance under that paragraph outside the Administrative Procedure Act. The 2022 workshop, REG-2022-00013, used the same disclaimer this invitation uses, and no title 10 artificial-intelligence section followed it.
4. Senate Bill 354’s revised Insurance Information and Privacy Protection Act becomes operative July 1, 2028. A regulation adopted before that date should use current sections 791.02, 791.09, and 791.10, and it should be conformed when the new article operates. The letter asks for that conformation. It does not ask the Department to apply the 2028 article now.
5. By February 15, 2027, the external events this letter can produce are receipt of the email and the December 15 workshop. A noticed proposal, a final statement of reasons, and a filed regulation are later events. No date for them is in the invitation.

**What the letter asks for, and why each piece can be audited.**

- A human-review record when an artificial-intelligence output is in the file for an adverse underwriting decision or a claim denial. An examiner looks for the name, the review of the output and the consumer-specific data, authority to change the action, and whether the person adopted the output, changed the action, or did not use it. A file that contains only a score fails. A medical-necessity determination already documented under section 10123.135 does not get a second reviewer.
- A version record made before that version is used: identifier, permitted action, data elements, date, person who accepted it, and either a comparison of the output to an outcome the insurer can observe on data not used to build the version, or a dated statement that no such outcome is observable yet. A changed version needs a new record. There is no passing score and no protected-class percentage. The invitation states no population, outcome, or disparity figure on which a number could be audited. Bulletin 2022-5 already points to the discrimination statutes. The record makes the reliability work examinable. It does not invent a fairness threshold.
- If a vendor supplies the system, the same two records have to be in the insurer’s possession. A contract clause that assigns compliance to the vendor is not the record. If the vendor does not provide the version identifier, the data elements, and the comparison, that version is not used as a basis for the action.
- If the insurer corrects an item under section 791.09, and that item supported an adverse underwriting decision that used an artificial-intelligence output, the insurer records a new review using the corrected item, or records that the decision does not depend on the item. Section 791.10 already requires the reasons and the supporting items. Section 791.09 already requires the correction. Neither section requires the decision to be reviewed again. The letter adds that link and does not add a new consumer-disclosure statute. It does not require an item that section 791.10 already allows the insurer to withhold.

**Personal information.** The invitation says the comment and the commenter’s address, telephone, and email become public. Put a name on the letter. Put an email only if it may be public. Leave the telephone blank.

**After a future notice.** Resubmit this letter, revised against the noticed text, during that comment period. Do not describe the December submission as the submission that the final statement of reasons must answer.

I am commenting on the questions in the November 13, 2026 invitation. I understand that this invitation is not a Notice of Proposed Action, that the Department is not required to respond to this comment, and that this comment is carried into a later rulemaking file only if I resubmit it after a future notice. The text below is offered for that future notice. It is not a request that the Department adopt regulatory text at the workshop.

The invitation asks what human review should occur, how insurers should know that a system remains reliable after deployment, what an insurer needs in order to oversee a third-party system, and how a consumer can correct an error. Bulletin 2022-5 already tells insurers that the Department may examine algorithms and models, and it already identifies Insurance Code section 791.10 as the source of the specific reasons for an adverse underwriting decision. The 2022 workshop, REG-2022-00013, asked how insurers validate third-party tools and how they state those reasons. No regulation followed. The gap that remains is a record an examiner can check.

I propose three records. None of them sets a disparity ratio, a pass rate, or a protected-class threshold. The invitation does not define a population or an outcome on which such a number could be audited. Insurance Code sections 679.71, 790.03, 10140, 1861.05, and the other statutes cited in Bulletin 2022-5 already prohibit unfair discrimination. A new percentage would not administer those statutes. A missing record would.

The records are documents about a system the insurer is already using for an adverse action. They are not a new rate filing and not a new consumer appeal. Insurance Code section 790.10 authorizes the Commissioner, after notice and public hearing, to promulgate reasonable rules necessary to administer the unfair-practices article. That is the proceeding in which this text belongs.

### 1. Human-review record

This responds to the invitation’s questions on human review, human judgment, and intervention.

When the file for an adverse underwriting decision, as defined in Insurance Code section 791.02, or the file for a denial of a claim in whole or in part, contains an artificial-intelligence output or identifies the system version that produced an output used in reaching the action, the insurer keeps a record in that file stating all of the following:

1. The name of the person who reviewed the action before the insurer communicated it.
2. That the person reviewed the output and the consumer-specific data the system used to produce the output.
3. That the person had authority to change the action.
4. Whether the person adopted the output, changed the action, or did not use the output as a basis for the action.

An examiner compares the file with the record. A file that contains a score, and none of the four items, fails. A file that contains the four items can be checked against the action that was communicated. The record does not require the person to be a particular vendor’s employee, and it does not require the insurer to disclose model weights.

This record is not required for a determination of medical necessity that is made, and documented, by a licensed physician or a licensed health care professional competent to evaluate the clinical issues, as Insurance Code section 10123.135 already requires. That statute, and the guidance issued under section 10123.135(j)(5), stay where they are. The record does not add a second reviewer on top of the licensed professional.

The record is an examination artifact. It does not change the notice Insurance Code section 791.10 already requires after an adverse underwriting decision, and it does not change the claim communications Bulletin 2022-5 identifies under Insurance Code section 790.03 and title 10, section 2695.7.

### 2. Version-locked evaluation record

This responds to the invitation’s questions on reliability, monitoring, and what insurers should know before deployment. The invitation states that a system may behave differently after deployment than it did during development, and that shortcomings in validation and monitoring can produce unreliable outcomes.

Before an insurer uses a version of an artificial-intelligence system as a basis for an action described in item 1, the insurer keeps a record for that version stating all of the following:

1. The version identifier.
2. The action the version is permitted to support.
3. The data elements the version uses.
4. The date of the evaluation and the name of the person who accepted the version for that use.
5. Either (A) a comparison, performed on data not used to build that version, of the version’s output to an outcome the insurer can observe, including the method and the result, or (B) a statement that no such outcome is yet observable, and the date by which the insurer will add the comparison in (A).

The insurer makes a new record before it uses a changed version as a basis for an action described in item 1.

An examiner fails the file if the version used for the action has no record, or if the record is dated after the action. The examiner can read the result of the comparison. The regulation would not declare that result pass or fail, and it would not set a number. If a later notice proposes a numeric standard, that standard needs a defined population, a defined outcome, and a stated comparison. This comment does not supply those, because the invitation does not contain them.

A statement under (B) is allowed only while the outcome cannot yet be observed. It has a date. An open-ended statement that the insurer will evaluate the version at some later time does not satisfy (B).

### 3. Third-party systems, and a new review after a correction

This responds to the invitation’s questions on third-party systems and on identifying, investigating, and correcting problems.

If a person other than the insurer supplies the system, the insurer still keeps the records in items 1 and 2. Those records are in the insurer’s possession. A contract term that places responsibility for the system on the supplier is not a substitute. The insurer may obtain the version identifier, the data elements, and the comparison from the supplier. The insurer does not need the supplier’s model weights or source code to keep the record. If the supplier does not provide the version identifier, the data elements, and the comparison, the insurer does not use that version as a basis for an action described in item 1.

An examiner looks in the insurer’s file. The examiner does not have to accept a clause that names the vendor as the responsible person.

Insurance Code section 791.10 already requires, after an adverse underwriting decision, the specific reasons and the specific items of personal and privileged information that support those reasons, subject to the exceptions in that section, including the exception for privileged information when the insurer has a reasonable suspicion of fraud, material misrepresentation, or material nondisclosure. Section 791.09 already requires the insurer, within 30 business days of a written request, to correct, amend, or delete disputed recorded personal information, or to refuse in writing and state the reasons. Neither section requires the insurer to review the adverse underwriting decision again after a correction of an item that supported it.

Where the insurer corrects, amends, or deletes an item under section 791.09, that item is one of the specific items that supported an adverse underwriting decision under section 791.10, and an artificial-intelligence output was a basis for that decision, the insurer, before leaving the decision in effect, either records a new review under item 1 that uses the corrected item, or records that the decision does not depend on the corrected item. The insurer furnishes to the consumer only what section 791.10 already requires it to furnish. This paragraph does not apply to an item the insurer is permitted to withhold under section 791.10(b)(2).

Senate Bill 354, Chapter 481, Statutes of 2026, revises this article operative July 1, 2028. Any regulation should use the correction and adverse-underwriting sections that are operative on the date of the action, and it should be conformed when the new article operates. This comment does not ask the Department to apply the 2028 article before its operative date.

### What I am not asking

I am not asking for a numeric fairness test, a prohibition on a class of models, a new consumer complaint bureau, or a change to section 10123.135. I am not asking the Department to treat this workshop comment as a comment on proposed regulatory text. If the Department issues a notice of proposed action, I will resubmit the portion of this comment that still applies to the noticed text.

Authority for a future regulation on these records: Insurance Code section 790.10, in a proceeding that also respects sections 791.02, 791.09, 791.10, and 10123.135.
