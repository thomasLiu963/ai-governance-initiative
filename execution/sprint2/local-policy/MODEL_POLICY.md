# Reusable AI safeguard clauses

Research date: 2026-11-19. These clauses are local proposals. No institution has adopted them. They are written so a later filing can copy one clause into a student-government resolution, a school-board policy, or a committee recommendation without pretending those bodies have the same legal power.

The clauses come from the gap already documented for UCLA on 2026-11-18 in `audits/university-policy/`: a detection score is not given an evidentiary value, the student is not told what must be disclosed before a penalty, and coursework can be uploaded to a detector the institution has not approved. The same gap appears in the California School Boards Association sample Board Policy 0441, whose operative language is a list of principles using "should," plus a direction that the superintendent keep AI use consistent with existing technology, honesty, homework, assessment, and student-use policies. That sample was read in the Visalia Unified first-reading packet of October 23, 2026, and in the Laguna Beach adopted policy dated June 1, 2025.

Education Code section 33328.5 required a state model policy. The Department of Education page that hosts it returned a captcha on 2026-11-19, as it did on 2026-11-18. These clauses do not quote that page. They also do not set a false-positive percentage, ban detection tools, or require a student to produce browsing history.

Each institution packet uses only the clauses that fit the decision in front of that body.

## Clause A — A detection output is not the sole basis

A score, percentage, highlight, or other output of a tool that classifies work as generated by artificial intelligence is not the sole basis for a finding of academic misconduct, a grade penalty imposed for suspected unauthorized use, or a disciplinary decision.

## Clause B — Notice and a chance to respond

Before a grade penalty or a disciplinary decision that relies on that output, the student receives the applicable rule, the name of the tool, the date it was used, the output, and a meaningful chance to respond, including with drafts or other process evidence.

## Clause C — The decision states the other evidence

The written decision states the evidence other than the detection output and states how the student's response was weighed. If the proceeding does not find the suspected misconduct, the work is graded without a penalty based on that suspicion.

## Clause D — The rule comes before the work

A student is not penalized for generative-AI use that the instructor or school had not classified as permitted, restricted, or prohibited, in writing, before the student undertook the work.

## Clause E — No unapproved upload

Student work is not submitted to a generative-AI detection service the institution has not approved for that purpose.

## Clause F — A person decides

A grade, a disciplinary decision, or a change in placement or program is made by a person. An artificial-intelligence output may inform that person. The person is able to reach a different result. The output does not itself assign the grade, impose the discipline, or change the placement.

## Clause G — The existing appeal reaches this evidence

The student or parent may use the institution's existing review, grievance, or complaint procedure to challenge a grade penalty or disciplinary decision that relied on an artificial-intelligence output. The reviewer states in writing the evidence other than that output.

## Clause H — An accessible way to finish the task

When the institution requires a student to use an artificial-intelligence tool for an assignment, and the student cannot use that tool, the institution provides a way to complete the same assignment without it.

## Clause I — The permitted use is stated where the work is assigned

The assignment or syllabus states whether generative-AI use is permitted, restricted, or prohibited for that work. Sample language that treats an undefined "confirmed" detector result as misconduct is withdrawn.

## What an adopter has to do

A student council that passes a resolution records its opinion and transmits it. The code or regulation changes only when the body named in that institution's own rules adopts the text.

A school board that adopts a board policy binds the district's students and staff to the extent the board's policies bind them. An administrative regulation, where the district uses one, is the superintendent's implementation of that policy. A county board policy binds the county office. It does not amend the policies of the school districts in the county.

A neighborhood council vote records that council's position. A community impact statement communicates the position to the City. It does not regulate a campus or a school district.

## Clauses considered and left out

A ban on all detectors. A numerical false-positive ceiling. A rule that a second detector's zero proves the student did not use a model. A requirement that a student disclose browsing history. A claim that any published error rate is the error rate of a tool a campus uses now.
