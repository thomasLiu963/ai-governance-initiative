## Authority and effect

This is a policy of the Tulare County Board of Education for the Tulare County Office of Education. The office operates its own programs and school sites, which its public site lists separately from the county's school districts. Adoption binds the county office to the extent a county board policy binds the office. It does not amend Visalia Unified's policies or any other district's policies.

A secondary summary, https://civiciq.com/public-project/at-its-november-17-2026-meeting-the-visalia-unified-school-c128965a-2f26-5066-ab59-ae9b90e0998d, reports that Visalia Unified adopted its own BP 0441 on November 17, 2026. The minutes were not opened. Visalia is not this packet. It is the reason this packet does not claim a countywide rule.

A letter does not put an amendment on the motion. A board member moves it. If December 11's agenda, when posted, does not contain BP 0441, the board cannot adopt it that day. The letter then waits for the meeting that does contain the second reading. The following regular meeting under the board's stated schedule would be the first Wednesday of January, which in 2027 is January 6, still before February 15. That January date was not posted as an agenda. It is the standing rule on the board page: first Wednesday, 3:00 p.m., unless otherwise indicated. December 11 is the indicated exception.

On November 4, 2026, the Board held a first reading of Board Policy 0441, Artificial Intelligence. Please add the following sentences to the policy before the second-reading vote. They fit after the policy's principles and before the direction to the County Superintendent.

"A grade, a disciplinary decision, or a change in a student's placement or program is made by a person. An artificial-intelligence output may inform that person, and that person can reach a different result. The output does not itself assign the grade, impose the discipline, or change the placement.

A score, percentage, highlight, or other output of a tool that classifies student work as generated by artificial intelligence is not the sole basis for a grade penalty or a disciplinary decision. Before either consequence, the student receives the applicable rule, the name of the tool, the date it was used, the output, and a meaningful chance to respond. The written decision states the evidence other than the output.

Student work is not submitted to a generative-AI detection service the County Office has not approved for that purpose.

When the County Office requires a student to use an artificial-intelligence tool for an assignment and the student cannot use that tool, the County Office provides a way to complete the same assignment without it. A student or parent may use the County Office's existing complaint and appeal procedures to challenge a grade penalty or disciplinary decision that relied on an artificial-intelligence output."

The sentences do not ban a tool. They state who decides, what a detection output cannot do by itself, what the student receives, and which existing appeal applies. Please include this letter with the December 11, 2026 materials if Board Policy 0441 is on that agenda, and with the second-reading materials if it returns at a later meeting.
