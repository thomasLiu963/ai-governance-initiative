# Ranking of institutional openings

Research date: 2026-11-19. Nothing in this file was submitted. No institution was contacted.

The rank is the chance of a documented institutional act by February 15, 2027, weighted in the order the sprint set: admissions-facing significance, chance of an affirmative adoption, predictability of the date, access, effort, and whether the same clauses can be reused. "Admissions-facing" is applied here as a posted decision of a UCLA or Los Angeles institution, in a process a student can enter, that a third party can read without taking the student's word for it. A mailed comment to a distant board can still be the right legal filing. It sits below a UCLA or Los Angeles vote when the two are otherwise close.

"Adoption" means the act that body's own rules treat as its decision. For USAC that act is the minutes. For a school board it is the vote that adopts the policy. A public comment, a survey, and a committee presentation are recorded separately and are not counted as adoption.

Twelve openings follow. The first three have complete packets.

## 1. UCLA Undergraduate Students Association Council

Packet: `packets/01-usac.md`.

The Council can vote a resolution before February 15. The target filing is Thursday, January 7, 2027, at noon, for a January 12 agenda, if that Tuesday is held. February 9 is the last Tuesday inside the horizon. The vote is a majority of the entire voting membership, with three Council sponsors. Passage is the Council's opinion. The Conduct Code and Regulation A-306 change only if the Chancellor and the Academic Senate later adopt text.

This is the strongest UCLA record available on a knowable calendar. The barrier is the three sponsors. None has been asked. A member of the public cannot file the resolution. The text already exists. The same clauses fit UCSA and the school-board letters.

November 24 is not the vehicle. Its noon deadline was November 19 and passed while this sprint was being written. The agenda had not been posted as of that evening.

Orange County, about 40,000 students in a district newsroom description. Operational if adopted. ## 7. North Westwood Neighborhood Council

The council's calendar page says the governing board meets the first Wednesday of the month at 7:00 p.m., hybrid, at UCLA Weyburn Terrace and on Zoom, unless a particular meeting says otherwise. Agendas for regular meetings are posted no later than 72 hours ahead. The next first Wednesday is December 2, 2026. After that: January 6 and February 3, 2027. Those dates follow the standing rule. A December 2 agenda was not opened, and for a Wednesday 7:00 p.m. meeting the 72-hour posting time is Sunday, November 29, at 7:00 p.m.

Bylaws approved April 1, 2024, opened November 19: the President sets the agenda. A majority of filled seats can require an item, in writing to the Secretary or by announcement at a Brown Act meeting, and the President places it on the next meeting or the one after. Any stakeholder may submit a written proposal or raise it in public comment. The board may consider it, may object to considering it, and is not required to act. Quorum is 10. An action passes by a simple majority of members present and voting, abstentions not counted.

A stakeholder is a person who lives, works, or owns property in the boundaries, or who is a community-interest stakeholder through a qualifying organization. Undergraduate and graduate student seats exist. Being a UCLA student is not, by itself, the stakeholder definition in the bylaws article that was read. A student who lives in the boundaries is a stakeholder under the "lives" clause.

A passed motion is the council's position. Los Angeles Administrative Code section 22.819, opened November 19, says a neighborhood council takes a formal position by a community impact statement filed with the City Clerk or by a written resolution, and a city board or commission agenda includes time for that position on items already on the city's agenda. No open City Council file on student AI detection or on a city AI policy was identified. Without a file, a CIS has nothing to attach to. A resolution aimed at UCLA asks a city advisory body to speak about a university rule it does not administer.

The council is the closest civic board to campus, the stakeholder path is real, and the product is advisory. It ranks below the bodies that can change a school or student rule. A packet is not warranted until a board member agrees to have the President agendize a specific motion, and until there is a city file or a UCLA question the council is actually considering. The Daily Bruin recap of the November 4 meeting shows the council passing letters and community impact statements on city matters. It does not show an AI item.
