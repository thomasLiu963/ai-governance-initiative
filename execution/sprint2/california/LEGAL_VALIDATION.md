# Legal validation

Date: 2026-11-19. Research memo, not a legal opinion. The two petitions were attacked on the grounds below before they were left standing. Closed leads are validated only to the extent needed to show why they were closed. Sources are the ones named in `STATEWIDE_SCREENING.md`.

## A. Department of Motor Vehicles — section 227.02(v), (w), and (x)

### The text

From the department’s Order to Adopt, Article 3.7, posted as a current regulation and effective April 28, 2026 (OAL file 2025-0415-04):

- Section 227.02(r): a “Drivered Testing Permit” is form OL 315 (Rev. 9/2024). It requires an autonomous-vehicle test driver on public roads.
- Section 227.02(t): a “Driverless Testing Permit” is form OL 315A (Rev. 10/2023). It does not require an autonomous-vehicle test driver in the driver’s seat.
- Section 227.02(u): a “Driverless Testing Permit Application” is form OL 318.
- Section 227.02(v): a “Drivered Testing Permit Renewal Application” is form OL 311, submitted by a manufacturer that possesses a valid drivered permit, “to renew the Driverless Testing Permit pursuant to section 227.20(a).”
- Section 227.02(w): the driverless modification application “means a Driverless Testing Permit, form OL 318,” used for the changes in section 227.42(o)(1) through (7).
- Section 227.02(x): the driverless renewal application “means a Driverless Testing Permit, form OL 318,” “to renew the Driverless Testing Permit pursuant to section 227.20(a).”

Section 227.42, first paragraph: a valid drivered permit, form OL 315, “is a condition for maintenance of” a driverless permit, form OL 315A. Section 227.42(o)(1) through (7) lists changes of automation level, roadway type, speed, geography, days and hours, restricted conditions, and vehicle make or model. Those paragraphs exist. Subsection (w)’s citation to them is accurate.

Section 227.20(a) gives each of the two permits a two-year term. Section 227.20(b) says renewal is made by submitting the drivered renewal application, form OL 311, or the driverless renewal application, form OL 318. The sentence is ungrammatical (“every [permit] which expires … shall be made”), and it does not pair each form with one permit in a single clause. It does not say that form OL 311 renews form OL 315A.

A Westlaw display of section 227.42 on 2026-11-19 shows form OL 318 at Rev. 6/2026 and still states that a valid drivered permit is a condition of the driverless permit. The department’s rulemaking history for that display is a change without regulatory effect. The full current page of section 227.02 did not load. Cornell’s section 227.02 is the pre-2026 text. Its history note stops at a section 100 change filed April 5, 2022.

### The defect

Three definitions in one section contradict the permit sections.

Subsection (v) names a drivered renewal and then says the renewal is of the driverless permit. Subsection (x), the twin definition, says the driverless renewal renews the driverless permit. Only subsection (v) points at the other permit.

Subsections (w) and (x) say form OL 318 is a driverless permit. Subsection (u) says form OL 318 is the driverless application. Subsection (t) says the driverless permit is form OL 315A. Section 227.42(o) calls OL 318 the modification application and calls OL 315A the permit.

A reader who follows subsection (v) files form OL 311 to renew authority to test with no person in the driver’s seat. A reader who follows subsection (t), section 227.20’s form names, and section 227.42 files form OL 318 to renew that authority and files form OL 311 to renew the permit that requires a test driver. Both readings are in the adopted article. Government Code section 11349(c) requires a regulation to be written so that the persons directly affected can understand it. A manufacturer and the department are those persons.

### Why it matters

The driverless permit is the decision that a vehicle may operate on a public road without a person in the driver’s seat. Remote drivers and remote assistants, defined in section 227.02(uu) and (tt), are the people who remain. Section 227.42 withholds the driverless permit unless the drivered permit is current. If a manufacturer treats form OL 311 as the driverless renewal, the drivered permit can lapse while the manufacturer believes the driverless permit was renewed, or the department can treat the driverless permit as expired because the manufacturer filed the form subsection (v) named. Either mistake is about whether the vehicle is still authorized to operate with nobody in the seat. Passengers and other road users are the people exposed to that mistake.

The correction does not decide how many miles a manufacturer must test, what a remote driver must be able to do, or whether heavy vehicles may operate. Those duties stay in the sections that already state them.

### Authority

The Order to Adopt cites Vehicle Code sections 1651 and 38750 as authority for section 227.02, and section 227.00(a) states that the article implements Division 16.6, commencing with section 38750. The requested action amends definitions the department has already adopted under that authority. It does not ask the department to regulate a subject section 38750 does not cover.

Government Code section 11340.6 applies. The department is not a judicial or legislative body. The definitions are not a tax ruling, a rate, a traffic-control-device standard under section 11340.9(h), or an order to a named person. They are not only internal management. They tell every manufacturer in the state which filing renews which authority to operate on a public road. No statute found in this research restricts who may petition the department or replaces section 11340.6 for these articles.

The form-instruction exemption in section 11340.9(c) does not cover this amendment. The request does not change the layout of form OL 311 or form OL 318. It changes which operating authority the definition says the form renews.

### Attempts to defeat the theory

**The defined term’s title already says “drivered,” so the last clause can be ignored.** The adopted sentence contains both. The preface of section 227.02 does not say that a defined term’s title defeats the clause that states what the filing does. Section 227.20 does not say to ignore subsection (v).

**Section 227.20 makes the error harmless.** Section 227.20(b) lists both applications and both permits. It does not state that form OL 311 renews form OL 315. Subsection (v) is the sentence that states a purpose, and the purpose it states is the driverless permit. Leaving subsection (v) in place leaves the contradiction in the code.

**Subsections (w) and (x) use “permit” loosely for the filing that produces the permit.** Subsection (u) uses “application” for the same form number, and subsection (t) reserves “permit” for form OL 315A. The loose reading erases a distinction the same section draws two subsections earlier. The requested change is the word “Application,” so that (w) and (x) match (u) and section 227.42(o).

**A change from “driverless” to “drivered” is a policy choice.** The petition does not ask the department to allow more vehicles to operate without a person in the seat, or fewer. It asks the department to make subsection (v) name the permit that the defined term and the drivered form already name. If the department intended form OL 311 to renew the driverless permit, the petition asks for a written denial that says so. That intent would have to be reconciled with subsection (x), with section 227.42’s condition, and with the name of subsection (v).

**The August 2026 section 100 filing already fixed it.** The filing, as displayed on the Westlaw page for section 227.42, updated a form revision date in that section. It was not shown to amend section 227.02(v), (w), or (x). This is the open factual risk. The petition is written so that the nouns change and the revision date is left as it stands. The person who mails it re-reads the three subsections that day. If the nouns have already been corrected, the letter is not mailed.

**Title 1, section 100, is the only lawful path, or it is unavailable.** Section 100(a)(4), as quoted in the phase 1 memo from the copies fetched on 2026-11-19, allows a cross-reference or syntax change without an Article 5 proceeding only when the change does not materially alter a requirement. Pointing subsection (v) at the drivered permit could be treated as restoring the only reading consistent with the defined term, or as changing which permit a filing renews. The petition asks for the correction and leaves that choice to the department and the Office of Administrative Law. It does not assert that section 100 will be used.

### What a grant is

A grant under section 11340.7 is a written decision to proceed, or other action the department finds warranted. It is not the amended code. An Article 5 amendment still needs the notice in Government Code section 11346.4, adoption, and Office of Administrative Law approval. A section 100 submission, if the Office accepts it, is reviewed on the timetable in title 1, section 100. No example was found of this department completing either path for a petition of this kind. The phase 1 examples remain denials: Board of Parole Hearings, petition of Raymond Quintana, decision August 1, 2018, thirteen days after receipt; Office of Administrative Law, Gambling Control Commission petition received November 22, 2022, denied in the December 2022 decision; Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation, Mills reconsideration, denial dated July 29, 2026, published in the Notice Register of September 11, 2026. Those are evidence of written decisions. They are not evidence of an amended code section.

### Result that can be described truthfully

By February 15, 2027, the supportable result is a written denial or a written decision to schedule a hearing, if the letter is received in December 2026, and later publication of that writing in the Notice Register under section 11340.7(d). The admissions-facing sentence for a completed code change is reserved for the day the amended subsection is in the code. It is stated in `RANKING.md`. Using it before that day would be false.

## B. Civil Rights Council — section 11008.1(a)

The phase 1 memo is the validation. This pass did not find a later amendment. The attacks considered there still hold, and they still do not defeat the petition.

The sentence says “employment benefit” and cites section 11008(i). After the subsection relettering filed June 27, 2025, and operative October 1, 2025, subsection (h) defines “Employment Benefit” and subsection (i) defines “Employment Practice.” The Council’s final statement of reasons, as described in `execution/phase1/MASTER_RANKING.md`, shows the letter was chosen while it still identified the benefit definition. The letter was not moved when “Agent” was inserted as subsection (a).

**Attack:** section 11008’s preface, “unless the context otherwise requires,” lets a reader prefer the words over the letter. **Answer:** that is a reason the Council might deny the petition as unnecessary. It is also why two readers can cover different conduct. Sections 11009(f), 11013, 11016, and 11017(e) use the defined term. The petition asks for one letter to change and asks the Council to deny in writing if it meant the citation to control.

**Attack:** title 1, section 100, is the better tool, so a petition is needless. **Answer:** section 100 is available only if the Office of Administrative Law agrees the change has no regulatory effect. If pointing the citation at subsection (h) changes who is covered, Article 5 is required. The petition leaves that choice to the Council and the Office. Government Code section 12935(a) is the authority. Section 11340.9 does not remove the regulation. No statute found restricts who may petition the Council.

The 30-day decision and the February 15 limit are the same as in the phase 1 memo. An amended section by February 15, 2027 is not claimed. The Council’s last full proceeding on this subject ran from a May 17, 2024 notice to an October 1, 2025 effective date. That duration is not the duration of a one-citation change. It is a reason not to promise a code change inside this checkpoint.

## C. Attacks that defeated other leads

**Privacy Protection Agency, sections 7010 and 7200.** The seam is real and temporary. It ends January 1, 2027 by the regulation’s own text. A petition cannot outrun that date. Defeated on timing, not because the seam was imaginary.

**DROP proposal, section 7612.** A 30-day access cycle would have conflicted with the 45-day statute still displayed on the official code page. Assembly Bill 883, Chapter 507, Statutes of 2026, approved September 27, 2026, changes those intervals to 30 days effective January 1, 2027. The statement of reasons says that, and the chaptered bill says that. The attack succeeds. No comment.

**DROP ZIP-code example.** Stripping leading zeros looks like it would break a hash. The statement of reasons says the platform hashes the zero-stripped value because brokers store ZIP codes as numbers. No platform document was read that hashes the opposite string. The attack is not proved. No comment.

**FPPC section 18450.12.** Collapsing the two statutory prongs into examples, and exempting an obvious cartoon, is a reading of Government Code section 84514(d)(2)(A)’s “falsely appear authentic” element. The Commission adopted it in 2026. A petition would ask the Commission to relitigate a choice it just made, and sections 11340.6 and 11340.7 are not the Commission’s procedure under section 83112 and title 2, section 18312. Defeated on both the merits and the instrument.

**Senate Bill 947.** A different definition of “automated decision system” in the Labor Code, effective July 1, 2027, does not make title 2, section 11008.1 invalid. The statutes regulate different duties, and different agencies enforce them. No sentence was found in which the Council’s rule requires what section 1520 forbids, or the reverse. Defeated.

**Section 227.42 notwithstanding section 227.22.** The renumbering suspicion is plausible. It fails as a petition theory because the drivered permit, which section 227.42 requires as a condition, is a testing permit that already triggers pull-notice enrollment under section 227.22. The notwithstanding clause does not open driverless testing to a manufacturer that never enrolled. Defeated.
