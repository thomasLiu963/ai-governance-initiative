# California statewide screening

Date of research: 2026-11-19. This file records what was read across California agencies. It is a research memo, not a legal opinion. Nothing in it was mailed, filed, or sent to an agency.

The prior memo, `execution/phase1/CALIFORNIA.md`, examined the Civil Rights Council, the California Privacy Protection Agency, the Department of Insurance, and the Department of Technology, and it looked at one DMV permit section. That pass found one adopted defect: title 2, section 11008.1(a), cites section 11008(i) for “employment benefit” when subsection (h) defines that term and subsection (i) defines “employment practice.” This screen does not reopen that sentence as the statewide lead. It is kept as a separate small petition. The question here was whether any other adopted rule, or any open rulemaking, has a defect that is at least as concrete.

Two sentences of method. A lead was kept only when an adopted or proposed sentence cannot be followed together with another sentence in the same rule, or together with the statute the rule cites, and the agency has authority to fix that sentence. A missing program, a policy the agency already considered and rejected, and a statute that operates without a regulation were closed. Twelve provisions were examined closely enough to accept or close. Two survive as petitions.

## What was treated as a source

Operative or proposed text was taken from the agency’s own order, notice, or final text, or from the official bill display, on 2026-11-19. Cornell’s copy of title 13, section 227.02 still ends its history note at a 2022 section 100 change and is the pre-2026 text. It was not used as the current autonomous-vehicle code. Westlaw extracts were used only where a full page loaded or a search extract quoted the sentence under discussion. Where a later filing might have changed a form date and the full current page did not load, that limit is stated on the lead.

Government Code sections 11340.6 and 11340.7, the 30-day petition decision, and the examples of written denials are the text and the three Notice Register examples already fetched for `execution/phase1/CALIFORNIA.md` on 2026-11-19. They are not re-quoted in full here. New agency-specific limits found in this pass are stated below.

## Surviving leads

### S-1. Department of Motor Vehicles, title 13, section 227.02(v), (w), and (x)

**Instrument:** petition under Government Code sections 11340.6 and 11340.7. Draft: `petition-dmv-227.02.md`.

The department’s Order to Adopt for Article 3.7, posted as a current regulation at https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/order-to-adopt-article-3-7-regulations-pdf/ and linked from the department’s autonomous-vehicle regulations page, is the clean text of OAL file 2025-0415-04. The department’s rulemaking page states the effective date as April 28, 2026.

Section 227.02(v) defines “Drivered Testing Permit Renewal Application” as form OL 311, submitted by a manufacturer that holds a valid drivered permit, “to renew the Driverless Testing Permit pursuant to section 227.20(a).”

Section 227.02(t) defines the driverless permit as form OL 315A. Section 227.02(u) defines the driverless application as form OL 318. Sections 227.02(w) and (x) then define the driverless modification application and the driverless renewal application as “a Driverless Testing Permit, form OL 318.”

Section 227.42’s opening paragraph states that a valid drivered permit, form OL 315, “is a condition for maintenance of” the driverless permit, form OL 315A. Section 227.42(o)(1) through (7) exists and lists the operational changes made on the modification application. The cross-reference in subsection (w) to those paragraphs is not broken.

The defect is the set of three definitions. The drivered renewal form is aimed at the driverless permit, and the two driverless applications are defined as the permit itself. Section 227.20(b) lists OL 311 and OL 318 by their application names and lists OL 315 and OL 315A as the permits. It does not rescue subsection (v).

A later section 100 filing updated form OL 318 to Rev. 6/2026 in section 227.42, as displayed on the Westlaw page for that section on 2026-11-19. That page was not a full copy of section 227.02. Whether the printed code after that filing still contains the word “Driverless” in subsection (v), and the word “Permit” in subsections (w) and (x), has to be re-read on the day a letter is mailed. The Order to Adopt, which the department still posts as the current article, contains all three sentences.

Why the people on the road are affected, the authority, the 30-day decision, and the adversarial objections are in `LEGAL_VALIDATION.md`.

## Leads examined and closed

### C-1. Privacy Protection Agency, title 11, sections 7010 and 7200

Closed on the ground already stated in the phase 1 memo. Section 7200(b) delays the article’s requirements until January 1, 2027 for a business already using automated decisionmaking technology. A petition filed in November 2026 cannot produce an amended section before that date. After that date the two readings require the same conduct.

### C-2. Privacy Protection Agency, open Delete Act rulemaking (DROP audits)

This is an active notice, not a petition target. Notice published November 13, 2026. Hearing and written comments close at 5:00 p.m. Pacific Time on January 6, 2027. Text: https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/data_broker_updates_and_drop_audits_topr.pdf. Notice: https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/data_broker_updates_and_drop_audits_nopr.pdf. Initial statement of reasons: https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/data_broker_updates_and_drop_audits_isor.pdf. Authority cited: Civil Code section 1798.99.87.

Three apparent defects were tested against the statute and the statement of reasons.

The proposal changes title 11, section 7612(a), from access at least once every 45 days to access at least once every 30 calendar days. The statement of reasons says this restates a statutory 30-day duty effective January 1, 2027. Assembly Bill 883, Chapter 507, Statutes of 2026, was approved and chaptered on September 27, 2026. The chaptered text amends Civil Code section 1798.99.86 so that the access, processing, and renewed-deletion intervals are 30 days. The official code display fetched the same day still showed the 45-day text and a 2025 history note, which is what a non-urgency chapter looks like before January 1, 2027. The proposal matches the statute that takes effect on that date. It is not a conflict.

The proposed ZIP-code rule, keep the first five characters and then remove leading zeros, with the example that 00321-1234 becomes 321, is explained in the statement of reasons as the standardization the platform already uses so that a hash matches a broker record stored as a number. A comment asking the agency to keep the leading zeros would fight the match the statement of reasons is trying to produce. No contrary platform specification was read. The example is not, on this record, a broken hash.

Proposed section 7630(b)’s example, an audit period from March 1, 2028 through March 1, 2029, with the report due May 30, 2029, matches a twelve-month anniversary period and a report 90 days after March 1, 2029. Civil Code section 1798.99.86(e) requires an audit beginning January 1, 2028, and every three years after. The proposal’s first report date of November 1, 2028 for brokers covered in 2026 and 2027 is an implementation of that start date, not a contradiction of it.

No comment is drafted. An open comment period is not, by itself, a defect.

### C-3. Fair Political Practices Commission, title 2, section 18450.12

The Commission adopted section 18450.12, “Artificial Intelligence Disclosure,” filed so that it became operative October 22, 2026. The text on the Commission’s regulation index gives three illustrations of when visual or audio media would falsely appear authentic, or would cause a fundamentally different understanding, for purposes of Government Code section 84514(d)(2)(A), and it says disclosure is not required solely for visual media that is obviously an animation or a cartoon.

Government Code section 84514(d)(2)(A), fetched 2026-11-19 from the official display, already splits “entirely created” media that would falsely appear authentic from “materially altered” media that would cause a fundamentally different understanding when compared with an unaltered version. The regulation’s examples sit on top of that split. They do not cite the wrong subdivision. The cartoon sentence uses “solely,” which tracks the statutory requirement that wholly generated media falsely appear authentic. An obvious cartoon does not. That is an interpretation the Commission adopted in August 2026 after notice. It is not a sentence that cannot be applied.

The procedure also fails. Government Code section 83112 requires Commission regulations to be adopted under the Administrative Procedure Act sections 11371 and following, which are the 1974 section numbers. Title 2, section 18312, states that every reference in that section to the Administrative Procedure Act is to the act as of June 4, 1974, and its comment cites *Fair Political Practices Commission v. Office of Administrative Law*. Section 18312 contains no 30-day petition decision and no duty to send a decision to the Office of Administrative Law for the Notice Register. Sections 11340.6 and 11340.7 were added in 1994. They are the instrument this project uses. They are not the procedure that governs this Commission. The 1974 petition section was not re-read in full, so this memo does not say the Commission has no petition practice of any kind. It does say a 11340.7 clock is not a result this record supports.

### C-4. Department of Insurance

No adopted regulation on underwriting or claims algorithms was found in the phase 1 pass, and none was found in this pass. The department’s November 13, 2026 release states that the December 15, 2026 workshop is not a notice of proposed action, that no regulation is being adopted that day, and that written workshop comments are due December 12, 2026 at CDIRegulations@insurance.ca.gov. A workshop comment does not trigger the response duty in Government Code section 11346.9. Insurance Code section 10123.135(j) is a statute. Paragraph (5) places the department’s utilization-review guidance outside the Administrative Procedure Act. Bulletin 2022-5 remains guidance. Closed.

### C-5. Department of Managed Health Care, title 28, section 1300.70

Section 1300.70 is the health-care-service-plan quality-assurance program. The subsections read on 2026-11-19 require licensed professionals to participate in quality-assurance activity and require the plan to monitor whether utilization meets professional standards. They do not authorize a non-physician to decide medical necessity. Health and Safety Code section 1367.01, as amended by Senate Bill 1120 (Stats. 2024, Ch. 879), already states that an artificial-intelligence tool shall not deny, delay, or modify services based on medical necessity and that a medical-necessity determination is made only by a licensed physician or other competent licensed professional. The statute operates on its own. No conflicting sentence in title 28 was found. A petition asking the department to copy section 1367.01 into the quality-assurance regulation would duplicate the statute. Closed.

### C-6. Department of Technology, Statewide Information Management Manual

Same ground as the phase 1 memo. The manual is departmental policy. It was not shown to be a title of the California Code of Regulations. Government Code section 11340.9(d) removes internal management from the chapter. Closed.

### C-7. Civil Rights Council, housing regulations and Government Code section 11135 regulations

Title 2, section 12005 and the fair-housing article define adverse housing actions. The department’s housing page states that tenant screening, including screening by a third party, is covered by the Fair Employment and Housing Act. Title 2, section 14026, operative July 1, 2024, prohibits a covered entity from using criteria or methods of administration that discriminate in a state program. Neither article contains a broken citation to an automated-decision definition. The employment article’s automated-decision rules were not copied into housing or into section 11135. That is an absence of a parallel article. Section 14026 can already be applied to a discriminatory method, including a computational one. Asking the Council to adopt a second automated-decision code, for housing or for public benefits, would be a new policy. The Council has authority under Government Code section 12935(a). Authority alone is not a defect in the text it has adopted. Closed.

### C-8. Senate Bill 947, Labor Code section 1520 and following

Chaptered in 2026. The added part begins July 1, 2027. “Automated decision system” is a computational process, derived from machine learning, statistical modeling, data analytics, or artificial intelligence, that issues a simplified output used to assist or replace human discretionary decisionmaking and that materially affects a natural person. The Labor Commissioner enforces the part. The definition is not the Civil Rights Council’s definition, and the duties are not the Fair Employment and Housing Act duties. No adopted Labor Code regulation was found that contradicts section 1520. The statute does not have to be copied into a regulation before it operates. A petition to invent that regulation is a policy project. Closed.

### C-9. Assembly Bill 566 and title 11, section 7025

Civil Code section 1798.136, operative January 1, 2027, requires a business that develops or maintains a browser to include a configurable opt-out preference signal. The agency “may” adopt regulations. Section 7025 already tells a business how to honor a signal that meets its two conditions. No notice of proposed action amending section 7025 was found in the Notice Register materials read on 2026-11-19. A news account that the agency board had voted to begin a rulemaking is not a notice. Closed.

### C-10. Assembly Bill 1979, clinical decision support

Approved September 30, 2026, Chapter 854. Operative January 1, 2027. It amends the Confidentiality of Medical Information Act and adds Business and Professions Code section 22758.5. A licensed professional must be able to exercise independent judgment when care is informed by a clinical decision support system, and a facility may not use artificial intelligence to perform a clinical function that the law requires a licensee to perform. Enforcement is by the licensing boards and, for a chatbot covered as a health-care provider, by the medical-information statute. No title 16 or title 22 sentence was found that authorizes the opposite. Closed.

### C-11. Other 2025 and 2026 statutes with no regulation to amend

Senate Bill 53 (frontier-model transparency, Department of Technology recommendations, Office of Emergency Services reports), Senate Bill 243 (companion chatbots), Assembly Bill 489 (health-care titles), Assembly Bill 853 and Senate Bill 1000 (provenance), and Assembly Bill 1159 (student information, operative July 1, 2027) were identified from the Governor’s signing announcements, the bill displays, and the October 5, 2026 California Lawyers Association status chart. Each is a statute. None was tied to an adopted regulation that cites the wrong subsection, conflicts with the new statute, or makes a required step impossible. Senate Bill 53’s annual recommendation by the Department of Technology is not a California Code of Regulations section.

### C-12. Department of Motor Vehicles, title 13, section 227.42’s reference to section 227.22

The driverless-testing section says its requirements apply notwithstanding sections 227.04(b), 227.22, 227.24(a) and (b), 227.32, 227.34, and 227.36. In the Order to Adopt, section 227.22 is enrollment in the Employer Pull Notice Program, and section 227.20 is the term of the permit. An earlier draft number for the term section was 227.22. The notwithstanding clause might therefore be a stale number.

It does not survive. Section 227.04(b) already says a test driver is required except as provided in section 227.42. Section 227.42’s first paragraph makes a valid drivered permit a condition of the driverless permit. The drivered permit is itself a testing permit, and section 227.22 requires pull-notice enrollment before a manufacturer applies for a testing permit. Reading section 227.42 as setting section 227.22 aside does not open a path to driverless testing by a manufacturer that skipped enrollment. The clause is awkward. It is not the sentence that decides whether a vehicle may operate with nobody in the driver’s seat. It is not included in the petition.

### C-13. Civil Rights Council employment rules, other than section 11008.1(a)

The exclusion in section 11008.1 for spreadsheets and similar tools, “provided that these technologies do not make a decision regarding an employment benefit,” is consistent with the opening definition. A tool that only facilitates a decision and does not make one stays inside the exclusion. A tool that makes the decision does not. That was the Council’s choice in the final text. It is not a second citation error.

The Council’s rulemaking page still places an automated-decision approval sentence under the contractor heading and dates a filing January 7, 2026. The phase 1 memo found that the linked text is the contractor nondiscrimination clause. This pass did not find a second automated-decision regulation under that heading. Closed.

### C-14. Public Utilities Commission passenger service in autonomous vehicles

The department’s April 28, 2026 announcement and a May 2026 Covington description of the same rules state that the DMV articles do not replace the Commission’s separate requirements for passenger service. Those Commission requirements were not shown in this pass to be sections of the California Code of Regulations adopted under Government Code section 11340.6. No petition is proposed against a Commission decision or general order on this record.

### C-15. Education, accessibility, benefits, and consumer financial regulators

Title 5 was not found, in the searches on 2026-11-19, to contain an adopted artificial-intelligence section with a broken cross-reference. Government Code section 7405 and the disability access statutes operate without a defective regulation identified here. Unemployment and Medi-Cal eligibility rules were not shown to contain a sentence that authorizes an automated denial the statute forbids. The Department of Financial Protection and Innovation’s commercial-financing rules were not shown to contain an automated-decision contradiction. These are closed for lack of a located sentence, not because those programs are unimportant. A later pass that quotes a specific subsection can reopen one of them. This pass does not invent the subsection.

## What this screen does not do

It does not read every section of the California Code of Regulations. It does not treat a statute’s silence about algorithms as a regulation to amend. It does not file the two drafts. The distinction between a closed lead and a failed initiative is in `RANKING.md`.
