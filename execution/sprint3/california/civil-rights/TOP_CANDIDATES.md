## 1. Send if approved — Civil Rights Council, qualified-employee designation

**Proceeding:** Notice File Number Z2026-1006-03. Title 2, articles 6 and 11. Comments close at 5:00 p.m. Pacific Time on December 8, 2026. Hearing the same day.

**Correction:** In the initial statement of reasons, the definition introduced under the heading “§11035(p). Qualified employee” must itself be designated subsection (p), not subsection (s). Subsection (s) remains “self-confirmation,” as the express terms and the later heading of the statement of reasons already say. The cross-reference “subsection 11050(m)(1)” becomes “subsection 11035(m)(1).” The express terms stay on subsection (p) for “qualified employee.”

**Why this is the filing.** Proposed sections 11040(c)(3) and 11041(g) decide whether a worker who cannot perform an essential function is still entitled to a pregnancy accommodation or transfer by pointing at section 11035(p). The express terms put that definition at (p). The statement of reasons prints the same definition as (s), and it also puts “self-confirmation” at (s). Those two readings cannot both be followed. The fix does not add a new accommodation, a new medical-certification rule, or a new automated-decision prohibition. The package already contains those proposals. The fix keeps the definition the accommodation sections cite from being moved onto the letter of a different term.

**Themes:** Human oversight of a consequential employment decision, and accountability for which workers the accommodation rule covers. The same package also prohibits discriminatory use of an automated-decision system. This comment does not amend that prohibition. The citation error in section 11008.1(a) remains the separate petition already drafted. It is not this comment.

**Burden:** One letter. Re-read the statement-of-reasons page and the express-terms subsection the day it is sent.

**Checkpoint.** November 30: the draft can exist. The Council will not have acted, because the record is still open. February 15, 2027: a final statement of reasons, or a notice of modified text, can exist. An Office of Administrative Law approval of the articles by that date is not claimed. The Council’s last employment automated-decision proceeding ran from a May 17, 2024 notice to an October 1, 2025 effective date. That duration is not the duration of a letter correction. It is a reason not to promise a filed code section inside this checkpoint.

**If the Council corrects the statement of reasons,** the truthful description is: the Civil Rights Council corrected the statement of reasons in its pregnancy-accommodation rulemaking so that the definition of a qualified employee is title 2, section 11035, subsection (p), which is the subsection the proposed accommodation rules cite, and so that subsection (s) is not used for that definition.

That sentence is reserved for the day the corrected statement, or a final statement of reasons containing the correction, is public. Using it before that day would be false.

**If the Council says the express terms already use (p) and it will conform the statement of reasons,** that is the same correction. Describe the document that actually changed.

**If the Council leaves the statement of reasons on (s) and adopts the express terms on (p),** the truthful description is narrower: the Council adopted section 11035(p) as the qualified-employee definition and did not correct the statement of reasons that had also labeled that definition as subsection (s). Do not describe the uncorrected statement as an adoption of (s).

**If the only writing is that the comment was received,** say that, and nothing more.
