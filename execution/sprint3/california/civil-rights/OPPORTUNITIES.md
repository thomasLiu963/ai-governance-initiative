## Surviving

### CR-1. Civil Rights Council — initial statement of reasons, “qualified employee,” title 2, section 11035

**Instrument.** Written comment in the open rulemaking, Notice File Number Z2026-1006-03. Not a petition under Government Code sections 11340.6 and 11340.7. The text is already noticed. A petition would ask the Council to open a second proceeding about a definition the express terms already state. Draft: `comment-crc-pregnancy-11035.md`.

**Regulation and proceeding.** The Council proposes to amend title 2, California Code of Regulations, division 4.1, chapter 5, subchapter 2, articles 6 and 11. The articles govern leave, reasonable accommodation, and transfer for pregnancy, childbirth, or a related medical condition, and bonding leave under the California Family Rights Act. Notice published in the California Regulatory Notice Register, 2026, Number 42-Z, October 16, 2026.

**Operative proposed language.** Two documents, both posted by the Council.

Express terms, October 2026 PDF, page 5 of the extracted text:

> (p) “Qualified employee” means either:
>
> (1) an employee who, with or without reasonable accommodation, can perform the essential functions of their position, or if the accommodation is leave, who is reasonably expected to be able to perform the essential functions, with or without an accommodation, at the end of the leave; or
>
> (2) an employee who is unable to perform one or more essential functions if the inability to perform the essential function(s) can be reasonably accommodated through, for instance, the temporary suspension of the essential function(s) …

The same express terms, later on that page, designate a different term:

> (s) “Self-confirmation” means a simple statement where an employee confirms that they need, and that their health care provider has advised, a reasonable accommodation because of pregnancy, childbirth, or a related medical condition.

Proposed section 11040(c)(3) and proposed section 11041(g) tell an employer and an employee to decide whether the employee is “qualified” by using section 11035(p). The initial statement of reasons repeats that citation on pages 11 and 14.

**The defect.** The initial statement of reasons, page 6, headings and definition block:

> §11035(p). “Qualified employee.” The Council proposes to add a definition for the term “qualified employee,” defined as follows:
>
> (s) “Qualified employee” means either:

The statement of reasons then defines “self-confirmation” at § 11035(s). One explanatory document therefore uses subsection (s) for two terms. The express terms use (s) only for self-confirmation, and they use (p) for the qualified-employee definition that sections 11040(c)(3) and 11041(g) cite.

A second sentence in the same statement of reasons, page 3, says the Council will move “affected by pregnancy” to new subsection 11035(m)(1), and then says to see the explanation of new subsection 11050(m)(1). Section 11050 is the medical-certification section. It has no subsection (m). The explanation the sentence promises is the explanation of subsection 11035(m)(1).

**Why it matters.** “Qualified employee” is the gate for the new accommodation duty. Proposed section 11040(c)(3) says an employee who rejects every reasonable accommodation or transfer, and who then cannot perform an essential function, is not “qualified,” as defined in section 11035(p). Proposed section 11040(c)(3)(A) says an employee who meets paragraph (p) and who needs an essential function suspended must receive that accommodation if it is reasonable. If a later draft follows the statement of reasons and places the qualified-employee definition at (s), it lands on the letter already used for self-confirmation, and the two accommodation sections still point at (p). A worker, an employer, and a hearing officer would not have one subsection that both defines the term and is the subsection the operative rules cite.

The comment does not ask the Council to move the definition. The express terms already put it at (p). The comment asks the Council to make the statement of reasons say (p), and to leave the express terms on (p).

**Legal authority.** Government Code section 12935(a) authorizes the Council to adopt regulations that interpret and implement the Fair Employment and Housing Act. The notice cites sections 12945 and 12945.2 as the provisions being implemented. Section 12945 is the pregnancy-leave, transfer, and accommodation statute. The qualified-employee definition is how the proposal specifies who holds that accommodation right when an essential function cannot be performed. Government Code section 11346.2 requires the initial statement of reasons to state the purpose of each adoption and the rationale for the determination that it is reasonably necessary. A statement that assigns the same new definition to subsection (p) and to subsection (s) does not do that for this definition. Section 11349(c) requires clarity. The express terms are clear. The statement of reasons, read with them, is not.

**Supporting evidence.** The October 16, 2026 notice, the express-terms PDF, and the initial-statement PDF, each fetched 2026-11-19. Pixel and text checks of the express terms separated strikeout from added text. The medical-certification designation keeps subsection (l): the letter (p) in “(pl)” is the struck letter, and (l) is the added or retained letter. The qualified-employee definition in the express terms is a new subsection (p). Those two designations do not collide. The collision is between the statement of reasons and the express terms.

**Proposed amendment.** No change to the express terms’ designation of “qualified employee” as section 11035(p), and no change to the designation of “self-confirmation” as section 11035(s). In the initial statement of reasons, replace the “(s)” that introduces the qualified-employee definition with “(p)”, and replace “subsection 11050(m)(1)” with “subsection 11035(m)(1)”. The full comment is the draft.

**Procedural route.** Email or mail, as the notice directs, by 5:00 p.m. Pacific Time on December 8, 2026. The hearing is the same day at 11:00 a.m., in person in Los Angeles and by the Zoom information in the notice. The Council must consider a timely comment. If it changes the text, Government Code section 11346.8 requires the further notice that section specifies. Section 11346.9 requires the response in the final statement of reasons. This comment asks for a correction of the statement of reasons and for the express terms to stay as they are on this letter. It does not ask for a new article.

**Effort.** One letter. The text is drafted. A name, address, and signature are still required before it can be sent. Re-read page 6 of the statement of reasons and the qualified-employee subsection of the express terms on the day it is sent.

**Benefit.** An employer reading the accommodation sections is told to use section 11035(p). A reader of the statement of reasons is told the definition is subsection (s), which is also the letter for self-confirmation. Correcting the statement removes that split before the Council adopts the articles. The worker who cannot perform one essential function, and who is still entitled to an accommodation if the definition applies, is the person the split would affect.

**Hypothetical description, if the Council makes the correction.** This sentence describes a result that has not happened. “In its 2026 pregnancy-accommodation rulemaking, the Civil Rights Council corrected the statement of reasons so that the definition of a qualified employee is subsection (p) of title 2, section 11035, the subsection the accommodation rules cite, rather than subsection (s), which the same statement uses for a different term.” Do not use this sentence unless the corrected statement, or an equivalent passage in the final statement of reasons, is public.

### CR-X3. CalHR, title 2, section 599.963, drug and alcohol testing

Open. Comments close at midnight on December 28, 2026. Notice File Number Z2026-1103, published November 13, 2026. Authority cited: Government Code section 19815.4. Reference: section 19261.

The current section lists marijuana and cannabinoids among the substances for both random and reasonable-suspicion testing, and it confirms a positive drug screen by gas chromatography/mass spectrometry. The proposal forbids a test conducted to find non-psychoactive marijuana metabolites, treats a result that identifies only those metabolites as invalid, removes marijuana from the random-testing list, and limits reasonable-suspicion marijuana testing to psychoactive metabolites. Oral fluid testing is permitted when appropriate. Positive drug screens must still be confirmed by gas chromatography/mass spectrometry or liquid chromatography/mass spectrometry before a sample is reported as positive.

That structure follows Government Code section 12954, which makes it unlawful to discriminate because a drug screen found nonpsychoactive cannabis metabolites, and which allows a scientifically valid screen that does not look for those metabolites. The statement of reasons says there is no accepted oral-fluid cutoff that demonstrates impairment, and that an oral-fluid result will not be used by itself as proof of impairment. The proposed text does not repeat that last sentence. Adding a mandatory second finding of impairment would be a new requirement. Section 12954 does not impose it, and the confirmation step the regulation already requires is the check the section does impose. No comment is drafted.

The breath-alcohol citations are not stale. Title 17, sections 1221.1, 1221.2, and 1221.3 were in force on the Cornell and Westlaw displays read on 2026-11-19. The current section 599.963 cites only 1221.2 and 1221.3. Adding 1221.1 adds the authorized-procedure section. It does not cite a repealed section.

### CR-X4. Housing, title 2, sections 12005, 12062, 12266, and 12269

Section 12266 requires a criminal-history practice that has a discriminatory effect to use only directly-related convictions as defined in section 12005(k), subject to the exclusions in section 12269, and it incorporates the justification elements of section 12062. Those subsections exist and say what the citation uses them for. Section 12005(k) is the directly-related-conviction definition. Section 12269(a) lists the records that may not be used, including arrests that did not result in a conviction.

The Council’s October 6, 2025 minutes record a unanimous vote to disband the Automated Decision-Making Systems and Housing Subcommittee. The employment automated-decision article was not copied into the housing article. Sprint 2 closed that absence because it is not a defect in the housing text. This pass has no new housing sentence that fails. A petition to write a housing automated-decision code would be a new policy, filed with a council that has disbanded the subcommittee created to consider it. No petition is drafted.

### CR-X5. Labor Code section 1520 and following, Senate Bill 947

Chaptered September 30, 2026. Operative July 1, 2027. The part restricts employer use of an automated decision system, requires human corroboration when an employer primarily relies on such a system for a disciplinary or termination decision, and requires a written post-use notice. No adopted regulation was found that commands the contrary. The duties do not wait for a regulation. A petition asking the Labor Commissioner to copy the part into a regulation before July 1, 2027 is a policy project. Senate Bill 7, a broader 2025 bill on the same subject, was vetoed on October 13, 2025, and the veto was sustained on March 2, 2026. It is not law. No comment and no petition are drafted.

### CR-X7. Professional licensing and clinical decisions

Assembly Bill 1979 is operative January 1, 2027. It requires a licensed professional to be able to exercise independent judgment when care is informed by a clinical decision support system. Sprint 2 found no title 16 or title 22 sentence authorizing the opposite. The October 2026 licensing notices identified in the agency screen are about examinations, forms, infection control, fees, and retired status. They were not read as if they were clinical-automation rules. No licensing comment is drafted.

### CR-X8. Workers’ compensation utilization review

Labor Code section 4610, as amended, provides that a modification, delay, or denial based on medical necessity is made by a licensed physician competent to evaluate the clinical issues. The open Division of Workers’ Compensation proposal noticed on October 23, 2026 amends the medical-provider-network article. It was not shown to authorize a software denial in place of that physician. No comment is drafted.
