Data Broker Updates and DROP Audits

Public comment on the notice published in the California Regulatory Notice Register, 2026, No. 46-Z (November 13, 2026).

Please amend one cross-reference, and please correct one word in an example.

1. Section 7614(b)(2)(B)

Proposed section 7614(b)(2)(B) tells a data broker to report “record opted out of sale” when one identifier matches more than one consumer, and it says the broker does so “as required in section 7613(b)(2)(B).”

Section 7613(b)(2)(B) is not a provision of the current regulation or of this proposal. In the regulation effective January 1, 2026, the multiple-consumer rule is section 7613(a)(2)(B). Subsection (b) is the deletion and suppression rule. It contains paragraph (1) and does not contain paragraph (2). The initial statement of reasons for this proposal says the multiple-consumer duty remains at subsection (a)(2)(B), and it says the suppression-list duty is subsection (b)(1)(B). Proposed section 7632(e)(6)(E) already requires the auditor to test the opt-out “as required by section 7613(a)(2)(B).”

The status sentence should cite the same subsection:

“Record opted out of sale” when the data broker cannot verify the request because multiple consumers are matched to the identifier, and the data broker opted out from sale or sharing all the personal information associated with all matched consumers as required in section 7613(a)(2)(B).

The status is the report a consumer later reads. A citation to a paragraph that does not exist does not tell the broker which duty that status implements. Section 7632 and section 7614 should name one subsection.

2. Section 7613, hashing example

The current example in section 7613(a)(2)(A), which this proposal moves into the composite-hashing step, says “data of birth” and then says “date of birth” for the same field. Please use “date of birth” in both places. The formatting rule in section 7613(a)(1)(A)(iii) already says “date of birth.”

No other section is requested to be changed. The 30-calendar-day access cycle, the removal of the 45-day limit on changing list selection, and the ZIP-code standardization are consistent with the initial statement of reasons and with Civil Code section 1798.99.86 as amended by Assembly Bill 883 (Stats. 2026, Ch. 507).

Authority for the requested amendment: Civil Code section 1798.99.87.
