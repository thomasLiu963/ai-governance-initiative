# Agency screen — California privacy and consumer protection

Date of research: 2026-11-19. Research memo, not a legal opinion. Nothing in this directory was mailed, filed, or sent.

The question was which California agency can amend an adopted rule, or is now taking comments on a proposed rule, on privacy, data-broker deletion, automated consumer decisions, insurance decisions, licensing, or an opt-out preference signal. A statute that operates without a regulation was recorded and closed. An adopted regulation was not closed merely because it is already in force.

## California Privacy Protection Agency

The Agency adopts the California Consumer Privacy Act regulations and the Delete Act regulations in title 11, division 6. Civil Code section 1798.185 is the CCPA rulemaking authority, including subdivision (a)(15) on automated decisionmaking technology and subdivision (a)(18) on the technical specifications for an opt-out preference signal. Civil Code section 1798.99.87 is the Delete Act rulemaking authority. Civil Code section 1798.136(c), operative January 1, 2027, authorizes regulations to implement the browser opt-out-signal duty added by Assembly Bill 566 (Stats. 2025, Ch. 465).

The Board adopts the regulations. For the open Delete Act proceeding, the notice names Kevin Sabo, Legal Division, 400 R Street, Suite 350, Sacramento, CA 95811, regulations@cppa.ca.gov, (279) 278-5086. The November 6, 2026 staff memorandum on opt-out preference signals is from Philip Laird, General Counsel, and the lawyers named on that memorandum. Official minutes of the November 5–6, 2026 meeting were not retrieved. The Agency’s regulations page, read the same day, still lists opt-out preference signals under preliminary activities and lists only the data-broker package as a formal proposal.

Two proceedings matter, and they are not the same instrument.

The data-broker package is a notice of proposed action, Notice Register 2026, No. 46-Z (November 13, 2026), notice file Z2026-11. Comments and the hearing close at 5:00 p.m. Pacific Time on January 6, 2027. Government Code section 11346.9(a)(3) requires the final statement of reasons, submitted with the adopted regulation, to summarize each timely objection or recommendation and to explain the change or the reason for no change. That statement is a verifiable agency act. It is prepared with adoption. Adoption still requires a Board vote, Office of Administrative Law approval, and filing with the Secretary of State.

The opt-out preference signal package is a Board packet dated for the November 5–6, 2026 meeting. The draft notice still says “Notice published [date].” It was not in Notice Register No. 45-Z (November 6) or No. 46-Z (November 13). No. 47-Z had not been posted on the Office of Administrative Law’s November index when this screen was written. A comment sent to the preliminary-comment address would not be a comment on a noticed proposal.

The adopted CCPA regulations, effective January 1, 2026, remain amendable. The compliance-date sentence in title 11, section 7200(b), is a separate question and is treated in `OPPORTUNITIES.md`. One adopted citation error does not depend on that sentence: section 7026(g) cites section 7025(b)(6) for the “Opt-Out Request Honored” example, and section 7025(b) has no paragraph (6). The display rule is section 7025(c)(6). That citation is a petition candidate if the opt-out package is not noticed. It is a comment candidate once the package is noticed, because the package already opens section 7026.

## Department of Insurance

The Commissioner adopts title 10 regulations under the Administrative Procedure Act. The Department’s rulemaking page, as read for the phase 1 memo on 2026-11-19, states that a petition under Government Code section 11340.6 is available and that the Department responds within 30 days. Insurance Code section 12921.7 adds emergency-regulation notice. It does not replace section 11340.6.

No adopted title 10 section on underwriting algorithms, claims algorithms, or automated consumer communications was located. Bulletin 2022-5 is guidance. Insurance Code section 10123.135(j) is a statute on utilization-review software. Paragraph (5) authorizes Department guidance and states that the guidance is not subject to the Administrative Procedure Act. A section 11340.6 petition cannot amend the statute and cannot amend guidance the statute has placed outside the chapter.

The Department has opened a pre-rulemaking record. Invitation REG-2026-00007, November 13, 2026, sets a virtual workshop for December 15, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. Pacific Time, and asks for written comments by December 12, 2026, at CDIRegulations@insurance.ca.gov. The invitation and the Department’s release of the same day state that the workshop is not a notice of proposed action and that no regulation is being adopted that day. A workshop comment becomes a public record. It does not trigger the response duty in Government Code section 11346.9. The Commissioner is the decision-maker for any later regulation. No later notice was in the November 6 or November 13 registers.

This is not a finding that the Department lacks authority. It is a finding that there is no adopted sentence to correct and no noticed text to comment on. A petition asking the Department to write a new underwriting code would be a request to start a program the Department has said it is still considering.

## Department of Consumer Affairs and licensing boards

Business and Professions Code section 4989.80 and following, the psychotherapy artificial-intelligence chapter in Senate Bill 903, was vetoed on September 30, 2026. The bill is not law, and no board regulation implementing it was found.

Assembly Bill 489 (Stats. 2025, Ch. 615) is in force as of January 1, 2026. It makes specified uses of a health-care title by an artificial-intelligence system enforceable by the licensing boards, including the Board of Psychology. The Board’s advisory describes the statute. No title 16 section was found that authorizes the representation the statute forbids.

Assembly Bill 1979 is operative January 1, 2027. It requires a licensed professional to be able to exercise independent judgment when care is informed by a clinical decision support system. Sprint 2 found no contrary title 16 or title 22 sentence. This pass did not find one either.

Senate Bill 243 (Stats. 2025, Ch. 677) added Business and Professions Code sections 22601 through 22603, operative January 1, 2026. Companion-chatbot operators have disclosure and safety duties. Annual reports to the Office of Suicide Prevention begin July 1, 2027. No notice of proposed action implementing that chapter was in the November registers read here. The statute does not assign the rule to the Privacy Protection Agency.

Government Code section 11546.45.5 requires the Department of Technology to inventory high-risk automated decision systems used by state agencies, including boards under the Department of Consumer Affairs. That inventory is not a title of the California Code of Regulations.

## Attorney General

The Attorney General enforces the California Consumer Privacy Act with the Agency and brings unfair-competition cases. The opt-out statement of reasons cites those cases, including the Disney complaint filed February 11, 2026, as the reason for the pseudonymous-profile examples. The consumer-privacy regulations themselves are the Agency’s title 11 regulations. No separate Attorney General regulation on opt-out signals, automated consumer decisions, or data brokers was in Notice Register No. 45-Z or No. 46-Z.

## Department of Financial Protection and Innovation

Title 10, the franchise-broker registration proposal, notice file Z2026-1027-04, is in Notice Register No. 45-Z. The title is franchise broker registration and disclosures. It was not read as an automated-decision or privacy regulation. The Department’s second invitation for comments on consumer-reporting registration, PRO 07-24, closed on February 26, 2026. That invitation is not an open comment period.

## What this screen does not claim

It does not read every section of titles 10, 11, and 16. It does not treat a vetoed bill, a self-executing statute, or a workshop invitation as a regulation that can be amended this month. It does not file the drafts in this directory.
