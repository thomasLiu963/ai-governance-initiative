# Top candidates

Date: 2026-11-19. Research memo, not a legal opinion. Nothing in this directory has been filed. A draft is not an agency decision, and a public comment is not an adopted regulation.

Two instruments are ready. They answer different problems. One asks the Department of Motor Vehicles to amend operative definitions that decide which filing renews the authority to test an autonomous vehicle with no person in the driver’s seat. The other asks the Office of Administrative Law to repair an open draft so that one of the three filings the draft defines can actually be filed. A third text, the driver safety proposal, has concrete defects and a closed comment period. It is held, not sent.

The November 30, 2026 checkpoint can be met by the existence of these drafts. It cannot be met by an agency decision. The 30-day petition clock in Government Code section 11340.7(a) runs from receipt. The Office of Administrative Law hearing is December 9, 2026.

## 1. Department of Motor Vehicles — title 13, section 227.02(v), (w), and (x)

**Status:** existing candidate, rechecked. Still the lead petition.

**Instrument:** petition under Government Code sections 11340.6 and 11340.7. Draft: `packets/01-dmv-227.02-petition.md`. Do not email it. Government Code section 11340.85(b)(5) requires an agency to have expressly said that it accepts a petition by electronic communication. A comment address on a noticed proposal is not that statement.

**Text relied on:** the department’s Order to Adopt for Article 3.7, posted as a current regulation, OAL file 2025-0415-04, effective April 28, 2026. Subsections (v), (w), and (x) are quoted in `EXISTING_REGULATIONS.md`.

**Recheck of the later form-date change.** A Westlaw display of current section 227.42 shows form OL 318 at Rev. 6/2026. The Order to Adopt shows Rev. 2/2025. This pass did not retrieve a history note, a file number, or a register citation for that difference, and it did not find the amendment named in the extracted text of Register 2026, Number 32-Z. The nouns were not shown to have changed. “Driverless” is still the permit that subsection (v) says the drivered renewal renews. “Permit” is still the word subsections (w) and (x) use for form OL 318, which subsection (u) defines as the application and which subsection (t) does not define as the permit. The later display is a form-revision update to the extent it was seen. It is not a correction of the operating authority the definition names.

**Why it is substantive.** The driverless permit is the authority to operate on a public road with no person in the driver’s seat, and section 227.42 makes a current drivered permit a condition of that authority. The three definitions can be followed to two different filings. Government Code section 11349(c) is the clarity standard. Title 1, section 100, is available only if the Office of Administrative Law agrees the change has no regulatory effect. Changing which permit a filing renews may be a regulatory effect. The petition leaves that choice to the department and the Office.

**Themes.** Authorization: the filing that renews each permit is identifiable. Human agency: the manufacturer and the department can tell which authority is current. The people exposed to a mistake are the people on the road with a vehicle that is, or is not, still permitted to operate with nobody in the driver’s seat.

**What can be said truthfully.** If the letter is received in December 2026 and the department follows section 11340.7(a), a written denial, or a written decision to schedule a hearing, can exist before February 15, 2027. Section 11340.7(d) then requires publication in the Notice Register. An amended section 227.02 by February 15, 2027, is not claimed. If the department makes the correction, the accurate sentence is that it amended section 227.02 so that the application renewing the permit which requires a person in the driver’s seat is no longer defined as the application that renews the permit to test with no person in the driver’s seat, and so that the driverless application forms are no longer defined as the permit.

**Before mailing.** Read subsections (v), (w), and (x) in the current code that day. If the nouns have already been corrected, do not mail the letter. Leave every form revision date as it stands. Cornell’s section 227.02, and the Westlaw document that still opens “As used in this article,” are the pre-2026 text. Do not use them for that check. Use the department’s posted order and, if it can be opened, the current Barclays or Westlaw section that postdates April 28, 2026.

## 2. Office of Administrative Law — proposed title 1, sections 1, 6, 14, and 110

**Status:** new, and the open-comment lead.

**Instrument:** written comment in notice file Z2026-0928-03. Draft: `packets/02-oal-file-and-print-comment.md`. The notice invites email to OALProposedRulemakings@oal.ca.gov. Comments close at 11:59 p.m. on December 9, 2026. The hearing is that day at 10:00 a.m. The decision-maker is the Office of Administrative Law. Contact: Lindsey McNeill.

**Defect.** Proposed section 1(a)(4) defines “File and Print” in three ways. Path (B) is a regulation adopted under some or all of the Administrative Procedure Act and expressly exempted by statute from Office of Administrative Law review. Proposed section 110(b)(2) then requires every File and Print submission to cite an Administrative Procedure Act exemption and to explain how each change falls within that exemption. Proposed section 110(d) says the Office will file or print the change only if it falls within a statutory exemption. A filing that is exempt from Office review, and not from the Act, has no Administrative Procedure Act exemption to cite. Read together, path (B) cannot be completed.

Proposed section 6(b)(3)(F)(1) still describes that same category, regulations adopted under the Act but exempted from Office review, as a “Print Only” request. Proposed section 1(a)(4)(B) calls it File and Print. Proposed section 1(a)(7) limits Print Only to a regulation the agency has already filed with the Secretary of State. The initial statement of reasons says the Print Only definition is removed from section 6 because it belongs in section 1. The originally proposed text still contains section 6(b)(3)(F)(1) and (2).

**Replacement.** The comment asks for three coordinated edits. Path (B) submissions cite the statute that exempts them from Office review, not an exemption from the Act that they do not have. Section 110(d) authorizes filing of a path (B) action when that statute applies, and it does not reimpose Government Code section 11349.1 review on an action a statute exempts from that review. Section 6(b)(3)(F) stops labeling the path (B) category as Print Only.

**Why a comment is the better instrument here.** The Office is already revising these sections. A petition to open a new rulemaking would arrive while this notice is pending. The hearing and the written-comment deadline are both December 9, 2026, which is inside the February 15, 2027 horizon. The comment can be evaluated at that hearing.

**What can be said truthfully.** By February 15, 2027, the supportable result of sending the comment is that the comment is in the rulemaking file and that the Office has had the hearing. If the Office changes the text, the accurate sentence is the sentence that describes the text it actually adopts. This memo does not predict that adoption. Proposed section 45, the companion proposal on the same day, was read and does not contain a comparable contradiction. No comment is drafted on section 45.

**Themes.** Transparency: the name of the filing matches the statute the agency must cite. Authorization: an agency that a statute exempts from Office review can still get a lawful regulation printed, and the Office does not use this section to review an action the statute removed from its review.

## 3. Department of Motor Vehicles — proposed Article 2.4, held

**Status:** defects confirmed in the notice and the marked express terms. Comment period closed November 16, 2026.

**Draft:** `packets/03-dmv-driver-safety-comment.md`. The banner on that draft says not to send it unless the department publishes a 15-day notice that still contains the sentences.

The register digest says a representative’s hearing request can be made only by telephone. The express terms say a telephone request can be made only by the driver. The marked text uses “proscribed” for the list of permitted request methods. The marked text of section 115.08 cites section 115.06 after the same proposal repeals section 115.06. Those are clarity and cross-reference defects in a proposal about whether a driver can obtain a hearing and how electronic evidence is exchanged. They are not yet the code. A petition is premature. After adoption, if the sentences remain, a section 11340.6 petition becomes the instrument, and title 1, section 100, is a possible path only for a change the Office agrees has no regulatory effect. Replacing “proscribed” with “prescribed” changes which requests are valid if the word is applied literally, so section 100 may be unavailable. That choice is not asserted now.

**Themes, if the 15-day window opens.** Human agency and authorization: a driver, or the person the driver asked to appear, can tell which method of request counts. Transparency: the notice and the text describe the same rule.

## Comparison

| Path | Window | What a success is before February 15, 2027 | What it is not |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Section 227.02 petition | No comment period. The 30 days run from receipt. A December receipt can produce a written decision inside the horizon. | A written grant, partial grant, or denial, later published under section 11340.7(d). | An amended code section. |
| Section 110 comment | Written comments and a hearing on December 9, 2026. | A comment in an open rulemaking file, considered at a scheduled hearing. | An adopted regulation, unless the Office actually adopts the change. |
| Driver safety comment | Closed November 16, 2026. A 15-day modification notice is the only remaining comment door. | Nothing, unless that notice is published and the comment is then sent. | A petition against text that is not in the code. |

The petition is the stronger accomplishment if the measure is a written decision on an operative rule about who may operate a vehicle with no person in the driver’s seat. The comment is the stronger accomplishment if the measure is a change that can still be made in a draft the agency is already adopting, on a known date, before February 15. Sending both is consistent. They do not depend on each other. Neither depends on the Department of Technology manuals, which remain outside the code.
