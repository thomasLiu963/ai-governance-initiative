# Existing regulations

Date of research: 2026-11-19. Research memo, not a legal opinion. This file covers operative text. Proposed text is in `REGULATORY_NOTICE_SCAN.md`.

The question for each section is whether an adopted sentence cannot be followed together with another sentence in the same article, or together with the statute the regulation cites, and whether the agency can fix that sentence. A missing program, a manual the statute assigns to the agency, and a policy choice the agency just adopted are not defects of that kind.

## 1. Title 13, section 227.02(v), (w), and (x) — still a substantive defect

### What was re-read

The Department of Motor Vehicles posts an Order to Adopt for Article 3.7 as a current regulation. The order is the text of OAL file 2025-0415-04. The department’s autonomous-vehicle regulations page presents that order, and the companion order for Article 3.8, as the current regulations. The prior research recorded the effective date as April 28, 2026. This pass did not find a later order that replaces it.

From that order, the definitions that decide the two testing authorities are:

- Section 227.02(r). A “Drivered Testing Permit” is form OL 315 (Rev. 9/2024). It requires an autonomous-vehicle test driver on public roads.
- Section 227.02(s). A “Drivered Testing Permit Application” is form OL 311 (Rev. 2/2025). The department must approve it before it issues form OL 315.
- Section 227.02(t). A “Driverless Testing Permit” is form OL 315A (Rev. 10/2023). It does not require an autonomous-vehicle test driver in the driver’s seat.
- Section 227.02(u). A “Driverless Testing Permit Application” is form OL 318 (Rev. 2/2025). The department must approve it before it issues form OL 315A.
- Section 227.02(v). A “Drivered Testing Permit Renewal Application” is form OL 311 (Rev. 2/2025), submitted by a manufacturer that possesses a valid drivered permit, “to renew the Driverless Testing Permit pursuant to section 227.20 (a).”
- Section 227.02(w). The “Driverless Testing Permit Operational Parameters Modification Application” “means a Driverless Testing Permit, form OL 318 (Rev. 2/2025),” used for the changes in section 227.42(o)(1) through (7).
- Section 227.02(x). The “Driverless Testing Permit Renewal Application” “means a Driverless Testing Permit, form OL 318 (Rev. 2/2025),” “to renew the Driverless Testing Permit pursuant to section 227.20 (a).”

Section 227.42’s opening paragraph in the same order says a valid drivered permit, form OL 315, “is a condition for maintenance of” a driverless permit, form OL 315A. Section 227.42(o) tells the manufacturer to submit the modification application, form OL 318, for the listed operational changes. Section 227.20(a) gives each permit a two-year term. Section 227.20(b) says the renewal is made by submitting form OL 311 or form OL 318. It does not say that form OL 311 renews form OL 315A.

### The August form-date question

A later commercial display of section 227.42, Westlaw document ID481BF50488111F185A8890ED67EED18, shows form OL 318 at Rev. 6/2026 in the opening paragraph, and it still states that a valid drivered permit is a condition of the driverless permit. This pass did not retrieve the history note under that section. It therefore does not state a file number, a filing date, or a register number for that revision-date change. Register 2026, Number 32-Z, August 7, 2026, was searched in the extracted text for the Department of Motor Vehicles and for section 227. The extracted summary does not name this amendment. The quarterly list of regulations filed from July 1 through September 30, 2026, is announced in Register 2026, Number 45-Z. This pass did not read that list line by line.

What the two official-facing texts do show is a form-date difference, not a noun change. The Order to Adopt, which the department still posts, uses Rev. 2/2025 in section 227.02 and in section 227.42. The later Westlaw display of section 227.42 uses Rev. 6/2026. Neither display was shown to replace “Driverless” with “Drivered” in subsection (v), or “Permit” with “Application” in subsections (w) and (x).

The Westlaw document that still carries the pre-2026 section number 227.02, document I5B40E0E35A1E11EC8227000D3A7C4BC3, opens with “As used in this article” and the old autonomous-mode definition. The Article 3.7 browse list on that service still numbers the driverless-permit section as 227.38. Cornell’s section 227.38 still incorporates form OL 318 at Rev. 7/2020, and its history runs through a section 100 change in Register 2024, Number 12. Those copies are the pre-2026 article. They are cited so they are not used as the current code. They also show why a commercial database, read alone, is not a safe copy of Article 3.7 on this date. The department’s Order to Adopt is the text this memo relies on for subsections (v), (w), and (x).

### Why the defect is substantive

Subsection (v) names the drivered renewal and then says the filing renews the driverless permit. Subsection (x) says the driverless renewal renews the driverless permit. Only subsection (v) points at the other permit.

Subsections (w) and (x) say form OL 318 is a driverless permit. Subsection (u) says form OL 318 is the application. Subsection (t) says the driverless permit is form OL 315A. Section 227.42(o) calls OL 318 the application and calls OL 315A the permit.

A manufacturer that follows subsection (v) files form OL 311 to renew authority to test with no person in the driver’s seat. A manufacturer that follows subsections (t) and (u), section 227.20’s form names, and section 227.42 files form OL 318 to renew that authority and files form OL 311 to renew the permit that requires a test driver. Both readings are in the adopted order. Government Code section 11349(c) requires a regulation to be written so that the persons directly affected can understand it. The manufacturer and the department are those persons.

The driverless permit is the decision that a vehicle may operate on a public road without a person in the driver’s seat. Section 227.42 withholds that permit unless the drivered permit is current. If form OL 311 is treated as the driverless renewal, the drivered permit can lapse while the manufacturer believes the driverless permit was renewed, or the department can treat the driverless permit as expired because the manufacturer filed the form subsection (v) named. Either result is about whether the vehicle is still authorized to operate with nobody in the seat.

Changing “Driverless” to “Drivered” in subsection (v), and changing “Permit” to “Application” in subsections (w) and (x), does not change how many miles must be tested, what a remote driver must be able to do, or whether a heavy vehicle may operate. Those requirements stay in the sections that already state them. The change makes the definition name the permit, or the application, that the defined term and the surrounding sections already name.

Title 1, section 100, does not fit this change on the sentence this pass retrieved. Section 100(a) allows a change outside Article 5 only when the change does not materially alter any requirement, right, responsibility, condition, prescription, or other regulatory element. Pointing subsection (v) at the drivered permit can be described as restoring the only reading that matches the defined term. It can also be described as changing which operating authority a filing renews. The second description is a regulatory effect. The petition asks for the correction and leaves the choice of procedure to the department and the Office of Administrative Law. It does not assert that section 100 will be used. A form-revision-date update, of the kind the later section 227.42 display appears to contain, is a different change. The petition does not ask to freeze or to alter any revision date.

Government Code section 11340.9 does not remove the definitions. They are not only internal management. They are not a form or the instructions on a form. Section 11340.9(c) says the form exemption is not a limitation on the requirement to adopt a regulation when one is needed to implement the law under which the form is issued. The request does not change the layout of form OL 311 or form OL 318. It changes which operating authority the definition says the form renews. Vehicle Code sections 1651 and 38750 are the authority notes on the order. The requested amendment stays inside definitions the department has already adopted under that authority.

### Sentences in the same orders that are not the same defect

Section 227.20(b) says every permit “which expires pursuant to this section shall be made by” the manufacturer. The permit is the subject, and “shall be made” is the verb for an application. The sentence still tells the manufacturer to submit form OL 311 or form OL 318 at least 60 days before expiration and to pay the fee. It does not pair each form with one permit. It is awkward. It does not, by itself, send the drivered form to the driverless permit. Subsection (v) is the sentence that does that. The petition does not ask to rewrite section 227.20.

Section 227.20(a) says “Driverless Vehicles Testing Permit,” and section 227.20(c) says “Driverless Testing Operational Parameters Modification Application.” The defined terms are “Driverless Testing Permit” and “Driverless Testing Permit Operational Parameters Modification Application.” The extra or missing word does not point a filing at the other permit. Those phrases are not in the petition.

Section 227.20(c) restarts the two-year term of a driverless permit when the department approves a modification application. That is an operative rule about the length of the permit. It is not a mistaken cross-name. It is not in the petition.

The Order to Adopt’s opening of section 227.42 repeats “which is hereby incorporated by reference” once in the first sentence. The later Westlaw display of that section, in the extract this pass retrieved, has the clause once. This pass does not ask the department to delete a duplicate that the later display may already have removed.

Section 227.42 says its driverless-testing requirements apply notwithstanding listed sections that include section 227.22. In the Order to Adopt, section 227.22 is enrollment in the Employer Pull Notice Program, and section 227.20 is the term of the permit. The prior screening closed this clause because the drivered permit, which section 227.42 requires as a condition, is itself a testing permit that triggers enrollment. This pass did not find a new fact that reopens it.

Section 228.02(e), in the Article 3.8 order, places the quotation marks around “Deployment Permit Operational Parameters Amendment Application” so that one quote falls before the defined term and one falls in the middle of it. The “means” clause still says the term is a Deployment Permit Application, form OL 321, submitted by a manufacturer that already holds a deployment permit. The deployment permit itself is form OL 321A, in subsection (c). The punctuation is broken. The sentence does not call the application the permit. Title 1, section 100, is the plausible procedure for a punctuation repair that does not change the “means” clause. It is a smaller and different request. It is not added to the petition, so that a syntax correction cannot be treated as a decision on subsection (v).

Section 228.02(h) incorporates the definitions in section 227.02. The drivered and driverless renewal definitions therefore apply when a deployment section uses those terms. Fixing section 227.02 fixes that incorporation. Article 3.8 does not need its own noun change.

The OL 311 application guide, a departmental PDF, cites section 227.36 for the two-year permit term. In the Order to Adopt, section 227.36 is the test-driver training program and section 227.20 is the term. The guide is not incorporated by reference in the sections read for this memo. A wrong citation in an unincorporated guide is not a code defect. It is recorded so it is not mistaken for one.

## 2. Operative driver safety regulations

Westlaw’s display of title 13, section 115.04, effective July 1, 2024, still states the pre-proposal rule: a hearing request under Vehicle Code section 14100 may be made in writing, by telephone, or through the portal, and the request contains a list of identifying information. The authority note on section 115.00 in that display cites Government Code section 11400.20. That section exists. It is the authority to adopt hearing regulations. It is not a mis-citation of section 11440.20.

The contradictions described in the notice scan are in the 2026 proposal. They are not in the operative section 115.04 this pass read. There is no petition to file against the operative hearing rule on this record.

## 4. What was looked for and not found in operative text

This pass looked for an adopted sentence on remote-driver authority, incident reporting, heavy-vehicle routing, and the safety case that cannot be applied together with Vehicle Code section 38750 or with another sentence in Articles 3.7 and 3.8. The Order to Adopt contains detailed duties: a remote driver must be permitted, must be able to stop the vehicle, and must follow a law enforcement officer; a heavy autonomous vehicle is limited to specified routes; a modification of the driverless operational design domain requires form OL 318. Those are requirements the department adopted in the April 28, 2026 order. Disagreeing with the mileage thresholds or with the hours-of-service rule for remote drivers of heavy vehicles would be a policy comment on a rule the department has just adopted. No second permit-name swap, of the kind in subsections (v), (w), and (x), was found in the deployment definitions.

No adopted procurement regulation, accessibility regulation of the Department of Technology, or government-services automation regulation was found with a quoted contradiction. Absence from this search is not a certificate that no sentence exists. A later pass that quotes a specific subsection can reopen one of those topics. This pass does not invent the subsection.
