Re: Driver safety hearings, notice file Z2026-0918-01. Comment on proposed sections 115.04 and 115.08, to be submitted only on a 15-day notice that still contains these sentences.

I ask the department to make four corrections. Each one is a conflict inside the notice or inside the marked express terms. None of them asks the department to change who is entitled to a hearing under the Vehicle Code.

1. The notice and the text describe opposite telephone rules.

The notice in Register 2026, Number 40-Z, says the proposal “clarifies that a hearing request made by a representative can only be made by telephone.”

The express terms of proposed section 115.04(a)(2) say: “A request for hearing made by telephone can only be made by the driver themselves.”

A representative who follows the notice calls. A representative who follows the text does not call, because the text reserves the telephone for the driver. Please make the notice and section 115.04(a)(2) state the same rule. If the department intends the text, the digest should say that a telephone request can be made only by the driver, and that a representative uses the writing or the portal. If the department intends the digest, section 115.04(a)(2) has to be rewritten. I am not asking the department to choose a policy in this comment beyond making one of those two sentences match the other. The marked text is the sentence I ask the department to keep, and the digest is the sentence I ask it to correct, unless the department says the digest was the intended rule.

2. “Proscribed” reverses the list of permitted methods.

The reader-accessible express terms mark this as new text in section 115.04:

“Any request for hearing made in a manner other than those proscribed in subsection (a) will not be considered a valid request for a hearing pursuant to this Article or Vehicle Code section 13558 or 14100.”

Subsection (a) lists the permitted methods: in writing, by telephone, or through the department’s portal. “Proscribed” means forbidden. A request made by a permitted method is a request made in a manner other than a forbidden manner. The sentence, read literally, rejects the methods subsection (a) allows.

Replace “proscribed” with “prescribed.”

3. The completeness test cites the form clause, not the list of information.

In the marked text, section 115.04(a)(1) requires the written request to be on form DS 2300. Section 115.04(a)(3) lists the information. Section 115.04(c) says a request that does not contain all the information specified in subsection (a)(1) is not valid. Subsection (a)(1) does not specify that information. Please cite subsection (a)(3).

The marked text of the change-of-information sentence cites subsection (a)(2). Subsection (a)(2) is the telephone limitation. It does not list information. Please cite subsection (a)(3) there as well.

4. Section 115.08 cites a section this proposal repeals.

The notice repeals section 115.06. The reader-accessible text of section 115.08 still directs a party to “electronic communication as outlined in section 115.06 of the is article.” Please delete the citation to section 115.06 and the words “of the is article.” If electronic discovery is still allowed, say so in section 115.08 without incorporating a repealed section. The marked text already says discovery may be served electronically. That sentence can stand on its own.

I am not asking the department to restore sections 115.14 and 115.15, to change the late-request standard, or to change the virtual-hearing rules.
