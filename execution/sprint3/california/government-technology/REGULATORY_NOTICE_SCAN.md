# Regulatory notice scan

Date of research: 2026-11-19. Research memo, not a legal opinion. Nothing here was filed.

The scan covered California Regulatory Notice Register numbers 32-Z through 46-Z, August 7 through November 13, 2026, using the Office of Administrative Law’s monthly tables of contents and the weekly issues cited in `SOURCE_LOG.md`. The next register after 46-Z is number 47-Z, scheduled for November 20, 2026. It was not published on the research date. Notices published before August 7 were not re-read unless a later register recorded an extension, a decision not to proceed, or a petition decision.

A notice published on a given Friday has a statutory minimum comment period of 45 days. The Office’s publication schedule for the second half of 2026 puts that minimum on these dates: October 2 closes no earlier than November 16; October 9, November 23; October 16, November 30; October 23, December 7; October 30, December 14; November 6, December 21; November 13, December 28. An agency may set a later date. The dates below are the dates stated in the notice, not the statutory minimum, when the notice was read.

The 2026 Rulemaking Calendar is a different document. It is incorporated by reference into Register 2026, Number 13-Z, March 27, 2026. The Office’s calendar page describes a 710-page compilation. This pass did not extract that compilation agency by agency. A calendar entry is a projection under Government Code section 11017.6. It is not a notice of proposed action and it does not open a comment period.

## Closed, with a defect worth keeping

### Department of Motor Vehicles, title 13, Article 2.4, driver safety hearings

Notice file Z2026-0918-01. Register 2026, Number 40-Z, October 2, 2026. The department’s rulemaking page and the register both say written comments were due November 16, 2026. That date has passed. No hearing was set. Contact on the notice: Randi Calkins, Regulations Specialist, Legal Affairs Division, P.O. Box 932382, MS C-244, Sacramento, CA 94232-3820, LADRegulations@dmv.ca.gov, (916) 282-7294.

The notice says the department will amend sections 115.01, 115.04, 115.08, 115.10, and 115.13, and repeal sections 115.06, 115.14, and 115.15. Authority cited in the notice includes Vehicle Code sections 1651 and 1801.2 and Government Code section 11400.20. Section 11400.20 is a real statute. It authorizes an agency to adopt regulations governing an adjudicative proceeding. The citation is not a typographical ghost.

Three defects are in the register and in the department’s own express terms.

The register’s digest says the proposal “clarifies that a hearing request made by a representative can only be made by telephone.” The express terms say the opposite: “A request for hearing made by telephone can only be made by the driver themselves.”

The reader-accessible express terms mark this sentence as new text: “Any request for hearing made in a manner other than those proscribed in subsection (a) will not be considered a valid request.” “Proscribed” means forbidden. The subsection it points to is the list of permitted methods: writing, telephone, or the department’s portal. Read literally, a request made by a permitted method is invalid, and the sentence does not invalidate a method the subsection forbids. The unmarked express-terms PDF contains the same word. That PDF also leaves struck and new words in the same line, so the reader-accessible file is the one that shows the sentence is new.

The same proposal repeals section 115.06, the electronic-service section. The reader-accessible text of proposed section 115.08 still says discovery may be requested by “electronic communication as outlined in section 115.06 of the is article.” The citation points at a section the same notice repeals, and “of the is article” is not a readable phrase. Proposed section 115.04(c), in that marked text, tests completeness against subsection (a)(1). In the marked revision, (a)(1) is the form requirement. The list of information is (a)(3).

These sentences are proposed text. They are not the code. A Government Code section 11340.6 petition cannot amend a proposal. The 45-day comment period is closed. The notice says that if the department modifies the text, the modified text is available for at least 15 days and comments on the modification are accepted for 15 days. `packets/03-dmv-driver-safety-comment.md` is written for that window. It is marked so that it is not sent unless a 15-day notice actually carries these sentences forward.

## What the scan did not find

No notice of proposed action in Registers 32-Z through 46-Z proposes to amend title 13, Article 3.7 or Article 3.8. The Department of Motor Vehicles’ open proposal in that window was the driver safety hearing package, and its comment period closed on November 16. No notice in that window proposes a regulation of the Department of Technology. No notice proposes to amend the State Administrative Manual or the Statewide Information Management Manual into the California Code of Regulations.

The Department of General Services’ electronic-signature proposal is the government-technology item that came closest to an open procurement or forms rule. The September 25 register records a decision not to proceed.
