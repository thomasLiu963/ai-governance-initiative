# Statewide agency inventory

Date of research: 2026-11-19. Research memo, not a legal opinion. Scope is the Department of Motor Vehicles, autonomous vehicles and transportation, the Department of Technology, state procurement and automated government services, accessibility of government technology, administrative procedure, and other agencies outside the civil-rights, privacy and consumer, and health and public-benefits assignments.

A row is included when the agency has a statute, a code, a manual, or a 2026 notice that this pass actually located. “No regulation found” means this pass did not find one. It is not a certificate that none exists.

## Department of Technology, reassessment

The earlier finding was that the Statewide Information Management Manual is departmental policy and is not a title of the California Code of Regulations, and that Government Code section 11340.9(d) removes a rule that relates only to the internal management of the state agency from the Administrative Procedure Act chapter. This pass rechecked the statute and the manual. That finding holds.

Government Code section 11545(b)(2), read on the official display on November 19, 2026, assigns the Director of Technology the duty of maintaining the information-technology sections of the State Administrative Manual. The manual is the instrument the statute names. Technology Letter 25-01 uses that statute, together with sections 11546, 11546.1, 11546.7, 11549.63, 11549.64, 11549.65, and 11549.66, as the authority for SAM 4986. SAM 4986.1 through 4986.13, as announced in the letter and as compiled in the Judicial Council’s copy of the manual, tell state entities how to inventory, host, assess, procure, and train on generative artificial intelligence. They also require solicitation language that tells a contractor to notify the state if generative artificial intelligence is a deliverable or materially affects functionality, risk, or performance.

Two theories were tested against that record.

A petition under Government Code section 11340.6 asks an agency to adopt, amend, or repeal a regulation under Article 5. The manual is not a regulation in the code. There is no section to amend. Asking the Department to copy the manual into the code would be a request to begin a program the Legislature told the Director to keep in the manual. That is a policy project.

An underground-regulation challenge is a different instrument. Government Code section 11340.5, as stated from the official display in the phase 1 memo fetched the same day, forbids an agency to enforce a rule that meets the definition of a regulation unless it has been adopted and filed. The Office of Administrative Law has a petition process for that claim. Its rulemaking page records the underground-regulation petition regulations as approved on October 31, 2022. Register 2026, Number 36-Z, contains an Office determination on a corrections memorandum, which shows that the Office does issue those determinations. This pass did not read title 1, section 250, in full, and it does not draft a challenge.

The challenge would be a poor fit for SAM 4986 on the record read here. Section 11545(b)(2) tells the Director to maintain the manual. Section 11340.9(d) removes internal management from the chapter. Directions to state entities about how they procure and operate technology sit on that internal-management side of the line. Contract clauses in a solicitation are closer to procurement terms than to a rule of general application enforced against the public. The State Contracting Manual and the Department of General Services’ procurement authority are the instruments for those clauses. This pass did not find a sentence of the manual that is being enforced against a private person as if it were a regulation, while also falling outside section 11545 and section 11340.9(d). Without that sentence, an underground-regulation petition would ask the Office to decide a theory this record does not prove.

Accessibility statutes point the same direction. Government Code section 11546.7 is among the statutes the Technology Letter cites. It is a statute about agency websites. This pass did not find a California Code of Regulations section, adopted by the Department of Technology, that contradicts it.

## Procurement and automated government services

The operative generative-artificial-intelligence procurement rules this pass found are SAM 4986.9 and the State Contracting Manual provisions the Technology Letter incorporates by announcement. They are not in the code. The one noticed regulatory project that would have governed electronic signatures on state forms was withdrawn by the Department of General Services. The State Allocation Board’s mandatory online-portal proposal closed before this research date, and its text was not validated.

No adopted procurement regulation was found with a broken cross-reference, an impossible step, or a conflict with the statute it cites.

## Administrative procedure, and the choice between a petition and a comment

Government Code section 11340.6 lets an interested person petition for the adoption, amendment, or repeal of a regulation, unless a statute restricts the petitioners or prescribes a different procedure. Section 11340.7(a) requires a written denial on the merits, or a decision to schedule a hearing under Article 5, within 30 days of receipt. Section 11340.7(d) requires that written decision to be sent to the Office of Administrative Law for the Notice Register. A grant is a decision to proceed. It is not the amended code. An Article 5 amendment still needs notice, adoption, and Office review. A change without regulatory effect under title 1, section 100, is available only when the change does not materially alter a requirement, right, responsibility, condition, prescription, or other regulatory element. That opening sentence is the part of section 100 retrieved this pass.

Where an agency has already published a notice, a precise comment on the proposed text can be considered before adoption. That path is shorter than a new petition when the comment window is still open and the defect is in the proposed sentence. The file-and-print proposal is that case. The autonomous-vehicle articles are the other case: they are already in force, and no notice in this scan proposes to amend them. A comment cannot amend an operative section. A petition can ask the department to open that amendment.

Public comments are not adopted regulations. A petition decision is not an amended code section. Those limits are repeated in `TOP_CANDIDATES.md`.
