# Legal review

Date: 2026-11-19. Research memo, not a legal opinion. The Early Start comment was attacked on the grounds below before it was left standing. Closed leads are addressed only to show why they were closed. Sources are the ones named in `SOURCE_LOG.md`. Nothing was filed.

## A. The instrument

The Department of Developmental Services published a notice of proposed action on the Early Intervention Services regulations. The notice that matches California Regulatory Notice Register numbers 45-Z (November 6, 2026) and 46-Z (November 13, 2026) opens the written comment period on November 6, 2026, and closes it on January 7, 2027. The hearing is January 7, 2027.

Government Code section 11346.8 gives an interested person the right to present statements at the hearing and in writing during the comment period. Government Code section 11346.9 requires the final statement of reasons to summarize each timely objection or recommendation and to explain how the proposed action was changed to accommodate it, or why it was not changed. That explanation is a written response. It is not approval of the comment, and it is not the filing of an amended code section with the Secretary of State.

Government Code sections 11340.6 and 11340.7 are the wrong instrument while this notice is open. Those sections require a written denial or the scheduling of a hearing under article 5. The department has already scheduled the article 5 hearing. A petition asking it to amend sections 52161 and 52112 would ask for a second hearing on the same sections. The comment draft is not labeled a petition.

A second PDF on the department’s site, `NoticeOfProposedRulemakingAction.pdf`, states a comment deadline and hearing of December 17, 2026, at Alta California Regional Center. The department’s regulations index, the notice PDF linked from the Early Intervention page, and the Notice Register state January 7, 2027, at 1215 O Street. The comment uses the register date. A person who sends the comment should not rely on the December 17 PDF.

Authority for the regulations is Government Code sections 95009 and 95028, which the current notes of sections 52161 and 52112 already cite and which the notice cites. The requested edits stay inside sections the notice already proposes to amend. They do not ask the department to regulate a subject those sections do not cover.

## B. Section 52161 — certified mail and the federal citation

### The text

Current section 52161(a), Cornell display read 2026-11-19:

> Written notice shall be given to the parent of an infant or toddler, eligible or suspected to be eligible to receive early intervention services, a reasonable time before a regional center or LEA proposes, or refuses, to initiate or change the identification, evaluation, assessment or placement of the infant or toddler, or early intervention services to the infant or toddler and the infant’s or toddler’s family.

The express terms, pages 47 and 48 of the proposed regulation order, replace “a reasonable time before” with “by certified mail at least 30 days prior to,” substitute “Early Start Program” for “regional center or LEA,” and leave the note’s citation to title 34, Code of Federal Regulations, sections 303.403(a), 303.403(b), and 303.403(c)(1) and (2).

The initial statement of reasons, section 52161(a), says the certified-mail requirement aligns with sections 303.231 and 303.234.

### The federal sections

Section 303.421(a), eCFR display read 2026-11-19, requires prior written notice to parents “a reasonable time before” the lead agency or early intervention service provider proposes or refuses to initiate or change identification, evaluation, placement, or early intervention services. Subsection (c) requires the notice in language understandable to the general public and in the parent’s native language, or other mode of communication, unless it is clearly not feasible. It does not mention certified mail or a 30-day period. The department’s notice of proposed action says that expressly.

Section 303.231(a)(2) requires the Secretary to send written notice to the lead agency by certified mail, return receipt requested, before a final determination that the state is not eligible for a Part C grant. Section 303.234(c) and (e) require certified mail of the hearing official’s initial decision and of the parties’ comments in that proceeding. Those sections regulate mail between the Secretary and the state. They do not regulate mail from a regional center to a parent.

Section 303.403, current eCFR text, defines “destruction,” “early intervention records,” and “participating agency.” Subsection (c) is a single paragraph. It has no (c)(1) or (c)(2). The note’s citation cannot be opened onto a notice requirement. Title 20, United States Code, section 1439(a)(6) and (7), read from the Cornell United States Code display on 2026-11-19, are the prior-notice and native-language paragraphs. They sit in subsection (a). The note’s “1439(6)-(7)” omits that subsection.

### The conflict inside the proposal

Proposed section 52104(h) requires written notice of the individualized family service plan meeting “no less than seven calendar days from the scheduled” meeting “pursuant to the general notice requirements contained in Section 52161,” and it waives the seven days when the parent asks for a meeting on shorter notice. The initial statement of reasons describes the seven days as advance notice.

A notice cannot be given both seven days before the meeting and thirty days before the proposal to change services if the meeting notice is, by the proposal’s own words, the section 52161 notice. A parent-requested waiver of the seven days does not waive a thirty-day certified-mail rule in section 52161. The parent who asks to meet sooner, and the program that tries to honor that request, cannot both follow the two sentences.

“From the scheduled” meeting is also the wrong direction for a notice whose stated purpose is attendance. The comment asks for “before.” That is one word. It does not change the seven-day period the department proposed.

### Attacks

**Certified mail is an additional state standard, and the notice of proposed action already admits that section 303.421 does not require it.** The department may adopt a standard above the federal minimum when it has authority and the standard can be followed with the rest of the proposal. This standard cannot. Section 52104(h) incorporates section 52161 and then sets a shorter period and a parent waiver. The statement of reasons cites the Secretary’s mail to the state as if it were the parent rule. The department’s own notice says section 303.421 does not specify certified mail. The comment asks the department to keep the timing words that already match section 303.421(a) and section 1439(a)(6). It does not ask the department to ban postal mail. A program may still send mail. The regulation would not say certified mail is the way notice is given, and it would not set thirty days as the minimum.

**The note is not operative text, so a wrong citation does not matter.** The note is the department’s statement of the federal sections the regulation implements. A service coordinator who opens section 303.403(c)(1) does not find a notice rule, because that paragraph does not exist. Other notes in the same express terms already cite section 303.421. The department has the correct section in the package. Section 52161’s note was not updated when the operative sentence was.

**“A reasonable time” is too vague, and thirty days is the clarification.** The current California sentence and the federal sentence use the same words. The proposal’s seven-day meeting notice, with a parent waiver, is the clarification the department wrote for meeting dates. Putting a longer, exclusive method into the general notice section unclarifies the meeting section. If the department wants a record that notice was sent, the comment does not forbid a record. It forbids making certified mail at least thirty days prior the definition of the notice.

**Subsection (c) already protects parents who read another language, so the method of delivery does not matter.** Subsection (c) is kept. Certified mail of a notice the parent cannot collect, or cannot collect before the meeting, does not perform subsection (c). The comment does not weaken subsection (c) and does not add a translation technology rule.

**The verbs in the express-terms conversion read “proposeing” and “refuseing.”** The plain-text conversion of the strikeout file joins the remaining letters of “proposes” and “refuses” to an inserted ending. The requested sentence uses “proposes, or refuses” with the singular subject “Early Start Program.” The comment does not depend on whether the marked copy, read as a PDF rather than as concatenated text, spells the participle correctly. Either participle still leaves the thirty-day conflict in place.

## C. Section 52112 — the private-school citation

Current section 52112(b)(2) requires notice to the local educational agency of an individualized family service plan meeting “requiring the attendance of an LEA representative pursuant to 34 CFR 300.132.” The express terms still contain that sentence after the new transition-notice paragraphs. The note cites section 303.148 and, in the proposal, also section 303.209.

Section 300.132, eCFR display read 2026-11-19, is “Provision of services for parentally-placed private school children with disabilities—basic requirement.” Section 300.124 is “Transition of children from the Part C program to preschool programs.” Subsection (c) requires each affected local educational agency to participate in transition planning conferences arranged by the lead agency. Section 303.209 is the current Part C transition rule. The fetched current text of Part 303 contains section 303.209 and does not contain section 303.148.

A service coordinator who follows section 300.132 looks up private-school proportionate-share services. A service coordinator who follows section 300.124(c) or section 303.209 looks up the transition conference. The proposal amends section 52112 and leaves both the old operative citation and the old note citation in place. The comment asks for the operative citation to name section 300.124, and for the note to drop section 303.148. It does not ask the department to change who attends the conference or the ninety-day transition timing the proposal already states.

**Attack: section 300.132 once meant transition, so readers know what was intended.** The section number now denotes a different duty. The persons directly affected include service coordinators and school districts who were not practicing in 1999. Government Code section 11349(c) requires a regulation to be written so that those persons can understand it. The proposal is the department’s occasion to change the number. Leaving it is a choice the final statement of reasons would have to explain.

**Attack: the new paragraphs in section 52112(b) already cite section 303.401(d) and section 303.209, so the old sentence is harmless.** The old sentence is still a command if it remains in the adopted text. Two commands that cite different federal sections are not cured by one of them being correct. If the department’s strikeout, which the plain-text conversion does not show as deleted, removes subsection (b)(2) as it now reads, the comment asks the department to say that in the final text. The requested replacement is the number 300.124 in any sentence that remains and that tells the district to attend because of a federal section.

## D. Attacks that defeated other leads

**Title 22, section 51003, and an automated denial.** Section 51003(a) and Welfare and Institutions Code section 14133.9(d) assign the non-criteria medical-necessity decision to a Medi-Cal consultant or other professional medical employee or contractor. No sentence was read that says a software tool may make that decision. Defeated.

**Title 22, section 51000.30(a)(2)(C), original signature in ink.** Assembly Bill 564 was not chaptered. Section 14044.1 is not in the official article. Section 14043.75(b) authorizes provider bulletins for specified enrollment sections without a chapter 3.5 rulemaking. A petition to delete “in ink” is a policy request the Legislature did not enact. Defeated.

**Title 22, section 50179, and Welfare and Institutions Code section 14005.31.** The notice exception tracks a statute that authorizes all-county letters until regulations exist. The hearing cross-reference matches section 50951(a). Defeated.

**Health and Safety Code section 1367.01(k) and title 28, section 1300.70.** The prohibition on a software denial of medical necessity is in the statute. The quality-assurance regulation does not authorize that denial. Defeated, as in the earlier screen.

**Health and Safety Code section 1339.75 and Business and Professions Code section 4999.9.** Each statute states the duty and the enforcing agency. No conflicting code section was found. Defeated.

**The October 17, 2025 language-access policy.** Human review before an artificial-intelligence translation is released is already the policy. The policy was not shown to be a regulation. A petition cannot amend it. Defeated as an instrument.

**The vital-records petition.** Health and Safety Code section 102134(c) authorizes the diacritical-mark list without further regulatory action. The department’s October 20, 2026 denial says a section 11340.6 petition is not the procedure. The reconsideration hearing on January 18, 2027 belongs to that petitioner. Defeated as a filing for this project.

**Hospice, Z2026-1016-01.** The comment deadline, December 14, 2026, was read in the October 30, 2026 register. The proposed text was not. No defect is asserted. Defeated for lack of a read sentence.

## E. What a grant is

A change in the final text of sections 52161, 52104, and 52112, approved by the Office of Administrative Law and filed with the Secretary of State, is the code change. A sentence in the final statement of reasons that rejects the comment and explains why is the other written result section 11346.9 requires. Neither result is produced by preparing this memo. The notice’s effective period is one year from publication, Government Code section 11346.4(b). Publication was November 6, 2026. The department has until November 6, 2027, to complete the action and transmit it, unless it issues a new notice.

This research did not find a Department of Developmental Services final statement of reasons that adopted a commenter’s federal-citation correction on a measured timetable. No duration is stated for adoption. The Department of Public Health’s October 20, 2026 denial, twenty-nine days after a September 21 receipt, is evidence of a written petition decision in a health department. It is evidence about petitions, and it is a denial. It is not evidence that this comment will be adopted.

## F. Result that can be described truthfully

By November 30, 2026, the supportable result is a draft comment ready for internal review. The comment period is still open on that date. Sending it requires a separate approval and a named person.

By February 15, 2027, the supportable result, if the comment is received by January 7, 2027, is that the comment is in the rulemaking record and that the January 7 hearing has occurred. A final statement of reasons, and an amended title 17, are later events. This memo does not claim either will exist by February 15, 2027.

If the department adopts the requested text, the truthful description is: the Department of Developmental Services amended title 17, section 52161, so that prior written notice to a parent remains notice given a reasonable time before the Early Start program proposes or refuses a change, and so that the note cites section 303.421 rather than section 303.403, and amended section 52112 so that the transition-meeting citation names section 300.124 rather than section 300.132.

If the department does not adopt it, the truthful description is: the department’s final statement of reasons addressed a comment asking it not to require certified mail thirty days before that notice, and asking it to correct the citations to sections 303.403, 303.148, and 300.132.
