# Agency screen — health, aging, accessibility, and public benefits

Date of research: 2026-11-19. This file records what was read. It is a research memo, not a legal opinion. Nothing in it was mailed, filed, or sent to an agency.

The earlier statewide screen, `execution/sprint2/california/STATEWIDE_SCREENING.md`, closed Department of Managed Health Care title 28, section 1300.70, Assembly Bill 1979, and the health, accessibility, and benefits programs as a group, because that pass had not quoted a sentence that could not be followed. This pass quotes the sentences. A lead is kept only when an adopted or proposed sentence cannot be followed together with another sentence in the same rulemaking, or together with the federal or state provision the sentence cites, and the agency can change that sentence in a proceeding that is actually open or that Government Code sections 11340.6 and 11340.7 actually govern. A statute that already operates, a departmental manual that is not in the California Code of Regulations, and a policy the agency has already stated are closed.

One proposed sentence survives, inside an open Department of Developmental Services rulemaking. No section 11340.6 petition is drafted. The reasons are in `OPPORTUNITIES.md` and `LEGAL_REVIEW.md`.

## What was treated as a source

Operative statutes were read from the official display at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov on 2026-11-19, except where that display returned an error and a second public copy was used and named. California Code of Regulations text was read from the Cornell Legal Information Institute display and, where a full official page loaded, from the Westlaw display linked in `SOURCE_LOG.md`. Cornell is not the official code printer. A one-word defect that appears only on Cornell is not treated as adopted. Proposed text was read from the agency PDF. Federal rules were read from the eCFR or Cornell’s eCFR display on the same day. Notice Register PDFs were read from oal.ca.gov.

## Department of Health Care Services

Title 22, section 50179, is the Medi-Cal-only notice of action. The section read on Cornell requires a written notice of the action, the reason and the law or regulation that requires a denial, discontinuance, or increase in share of cost, the hearing right, and how aid is continued. Subsection (c)(4)(A) limits the hearing right “except as limited in Section 50951(a).” Section 50951(a) exists and states that limit: there is no hearing where the only issue is a correctly applied state or federal law that requires a reduction for a class of beneficiaries. The cross-reference can be followed.

Section 50179(b) excepts notices required by Welfare and Institutions Code sections 14005.31 and 14005.32 from the prescribed-form and worker-contact sentences. Section 14005.31 was read. It requires a specific continuation notice when CalWORKs cash aid ends and Medi-Cal continues, and subdivision (d) says the department implements that section by all-county letters “without taking any further regulatory action” until it adopts regulations. The exception in section 50179(b) matches that statutory notice. It is not a broken citation. The statute’s permission to use letters until regulations exist is not a sentence this pass can force into a petition on a measured clock. The same pattern is why the Department of Public Health denied the diacritical-mark petition discussed below.

Welfare and Institutions Code section 14133.9(d) requires a professional medical employee or contractor of the department to decide medical necessity when a treatment authorization request is not subject to objective medical criteria, and it requires authorization when objective criteria are met and the service is not contraindicated. Title 22, section 51003(a), assigns prior authorization to a designated Medi-Cal consultant and says that responsibility shall not be delegated except as sections 51013 and 51014 provide. Subsection (e) incorporates the “Manual of Criteria for Medi-Cal Authorization,” last revised May 1, 2013, as the basis for the consultant’s professional judgment on listed services. The statute and the regulation both put a person, the consultant, on the decision that is not resolved by the criteria. This pass did not find a sentence in section 51003 that authorizes software to deny a request the statute requires a professional to decide. Asking the department to add an artificial-intelligence prohibition would copy a duty the consultant sentence and section 14133.9(d) already state for fee-for-service authorization. Closed.

Section 51000.30(a)(2)(C) still says a provider application package shall contain an original signature in ink. Assembly Bill 564 of the 2023–2024 session, which would have added Welfare and Institutions Code section 14044.1 and required electronic signatures on enrollment forms, was not chaptered. The official article display of provider-enrollment statutes runs through section 14045 and does not contain section 14044.1. Welfare and Institutions Code section 14043.75(b) separately lets the director implement specified enrollment sections by provider bulletin without an Administrative Procedure Act rulemaking. The department’s Provider Application and Validation for Enrollment bulletin uses that authority for digital signatures on the providers it covers. The ink sentence and the bulletin are not the same instrument. A petition to delete “in ink” would ask the department to adopt a change the Legislature did not enact, on top of a bulletin statute that already bypasses the chapter for the providers it names. Closed.

Section 50951, as displayed on Cornell, still says “Department of Health Services,” the department’s former name, and displays “or he Department.” That one-letter display was not confirmed on the official code printer. A successor-agency name that a reader can still apply is not the defect this screen keeps. Closed pending an official-printer reading, and not drafted.

Welfare and Institutions Code section 14029.91 requires managed-care plans to provide spoken interpretation and a written notice of free language assistance. It is a statute. No title 22 sentence read in this pass says a plan may refuse that notice. All-County Welfare Director letters on notice language and type size are departmental instructions. They are not, on this record, sections of the code. Closed.

## Department of Managed Health Care

Health and Safety Code section 1367.01(k), added by Senate Bill 1120 and operative January 1, 2025, was read from the official display. A health care service plan’s artificial intelligence or other software tool used for utilization review based on medical necessity shall not deny, delay, or modify services on that basis. A medical-necessity determination is made only by a licensed physician or other competent licensed professional. Title 28, section 1300.70, read from Cornell and from the Westlaw extract used in the earlier screen, requires licensed professionals to participate in quality-assurance activity and requires the plan to monitor whether utilization meets professional standards. It does not say a software tool may make the medical-necessity determination. The statute operates on its own. Copying it into section 1300.70 would duplicate it. The earlier closure stands.

No open Department of Managed Health Care notice of proposed action on utilization review was found in the November 2026 Notice Register tables or on the Office of Administrative Law’s proposed-regulations list read the same day. Two file-and-print items, infertility services and health equity and quality, were on that list. File-and-print is not a comment proceeding on automated medical-necessity decisions. Closed.

## Department of Public Health

Health and Safety Code section 1339.75, added by Assembly Bill 3030 and operative January 1, 2025, requires a disclaimer and a way to reach a person when a health facility, clinic, or physician office uses generative artificial intelligence for a communication about patient clinical information, unless a licensed or certified person has read and reviewed the communication. The Medical Board’s public page restates the statute. No title 22 sentence was found that authorizes the opposite. The statute names the enforcement paths: facility and clinic licensing mechanisms, and the Medical Board or Osteopathic Medical Board for a physician. A regulation that repeated the statute is not required for the statute to operate. Closed.

The department’s hospice rulemaking, Notice File Number Z2026-1016-01, was published in the Notice Register of October 30, 2026. Written comments must be received by December 14, 2026. This pass read that deadline from the register. It did not read the proposed hospice text. No hospice comment is drafted. An open comment period is not, by itself, a defect.

Health and Safety Code section 102134(c) lets the State Registrar compile the list of acceptable diacritical marks on vital records by all-county letter “without taking further regulatory action.” On September 21, 2026, the department received a petition under Government Code section 11340.6 from Dr. Lucjan Złotnicki asking it to include the letters Ł and ł and to stop excluding them by guidance. The department denied the petition on October 20, 2026, because the statute places the list outside the Administrative Procedure Act. The petitioner requested reconsideration the same day. On November 3, 2026, the department set a virtual hearing on that reconsideration for January 18, 2027. Both writings are in the Notice Register of November 13, 2026. This screen does not file a second petition on the same list. The denial is used only as evidence of what this department does when a statute has already removed the subject from chapter 3.5.

## Licensing boards

Business and Professions Code sections 4999.8 and 4999.9, added by Assembly Bill 489, Chapter 615, Statutes of 2025, operative January 1, 2026, prohibit an artificial-intelligence system from using specified health-profession terms so that the system implies a licensed natural person is providing the care. Section 4999.8 defines artificial intelligence by Government Code section 11546.45.5 and generative artificial intelligence by Government Code section 11549.64. Both of those Government Code sections were read. Both contain the definitions the Business and Professions Code cites. Enforcement is by the licensing board. No title 16 sentence was found that authorizes the use the statute prohibits. Assembly Bill 1979 remains closed on the ground stated in the earlier screen: no conflicting title 16 or title 22 sentence was found. Closed.

## Department of Social Services and language access

The California Health and Human Services Agency’s Language Access Policy, revised October 17, 2025, was read in part. It says that if a department uses machine or artificial-intelligence translation for a vital document or essential public website content, a qualified human reviewer must review the translation for accuracy and quality before it is finalized or reaches the intended audience. That sentence is an agency policy. It was not shown to be a section of the California Code of Regulations. The safeguard the policy already states is human review before the translation is used. This screen does not propose a second version of that sentence, and it does not label the policy a regulation.

The department’s regulations-in-process page was opened. It is the index of packages. No open package read in this pass contained a proposed sentence on automated eligibility, notice language, or appeals that conflicted with the statute it cited. The semi-annual reporting notice published in the Notice Register of October 3, 2025, Z2025-0919-02, is outside any 45-day comment window as of this date. Its text was not re-opened as a petition because no conflicting adopted sentence was located. Manual of Policies and Procedures divisions are the department’s regulatory compilation for the programs those divisions govern. This pass did not find a quoted subsection that authorizes an automated denial the hearing rules forbid. Closed for lack of that sentence.

## Department of Developmental Services

This is the open proceeding. The department proposes to amend the Early Intervention Services regulations in title 17, including sections 52104, 52112, and 52161. The notice that matches the California Regulatory Notice Register is `NoticeProposedRulemakingAction_2026.pdf` on the department’s site. Comments close January 7, 2027. The hearing is the same day. A different file on the same site, `NoticeOfProposedRulemakingAction.pdf`, states a hearing on December 17, 2026, at a different address. The department’s regulations index and Notice Register numbers 45-Z and 46-Z state January 7, 2027. The comment draft uses January 7.

The express terms and the initial statement of reasons were read. Two groups of sentences cannot be followed together. They are the surviving lead. The comment is in `comment-dds-early-start.md`. Consent withdrawal is already in the proposed consent definition and in proposed section 52162: consent is voluntary and may be revoked, and revocation is not retroactive. That safeguard is not a gap. The comment does not add a consent rule.

A separate department reconsideration, about Gateway Transportation and service code 875, is noted in the November 13, 2026 register. It is a reimbursement petition. It is not this lead.
